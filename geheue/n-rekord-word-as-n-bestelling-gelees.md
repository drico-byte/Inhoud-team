My whole repair method rests on records: I rewrite the ordering sentence and keep the
old form below it, marked "TERUGGETREK, NET AS REKORD, BESTEL NIKS". On 29 September 2026
that label failed four times in one day. Two writers reported a field as still ordering the
withdrawn form when the field was already correct — they had read my own record. Two
checkers reported a contradiction in a field whose "replaced" half explicitly called its
items examples. And one of my own asserts fired on my note's quotation of the wording it
replaced.

**Why:** an agent reading a long field pattern-matches on capitalised, dated, imperative
text. The label is one clause; the old order is a whole sentence in the voice of an order.
The old *words* are the hazard, not their position or their heading.

**How to apply:** when a correction must keep a record, **paraphrase what it replaces
instead of quoting it** — "die ou vorm het die dader tot 'n kind beperk" rather than
reproducing the sentence. Quote verbatim only where the exact words matter for a sweep, and
then say in the same breath that nothing in the quotation is an order. Never test "is the
old order gone?" against the whole field: assert that every occurrence sits after the
withdrawal marker, or better, that the live form is present and the field reads as prose.
And when a writer or checker reports a stale requirement, **read the field before acting** —
it is roughly even odds the report is about my own record. See
[[die-korrigeerde-opdrag-bly-in-die-veld-staan]], [[n-laslap-binne-n-sin-breek-die-veld-as-prosa]],
[[n-teruggetrekte-beslissing-bly-in-hoofletters-staan]].
