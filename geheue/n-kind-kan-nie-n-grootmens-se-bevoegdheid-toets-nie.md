5 October 2026, Grade 7 LO, the lesson on body privacy and unwanted touching. One
sentence — which adult may look at or touch a child's private parts — took **three
consecutive rounds of correction, and the sixth fact check contradicted the fifth.**

- **Round 4:** the exception hangs on a job TITLE (doctor or nurse). A title is the
  easiest thing for an abuser to claim. Move it to a purpose.
- **Round 5:** a stated PURPOSE is just as claimable — the grooming research documents
  offenders working through exactly that. So demand the reason *and* the person *and*
  the place *and* that someone else is present.
- **Round 6:** the third-person demand is **false.** A chaperone is *offered*, not
  required, and the patient may decline it; section 129 of the Children's Act lets a
  child over 12 consent to her own treatment and receive it confidentially — which is
  exactly what that lesson's own last block invites her to do.

**Why it is worse than merely wrong: it failed in both directions at once.** A learner
lawfully seen alone at a clinic would have to conclude the care was wrong. And an abuser
who makes sure someone else is in the room *passes the lesson's test*.

**The constraint emptying the space was mine.** Each round I wrote a better credential
test, so each round found a better way to defeat it. A child cannot test an adult's
standing: every credential is claimable, and every real credential has lawful exceptions
that a rule tight enough to exclude the abuser also excludes. That is why the signal in
[[twee-vereistes-wat-net-saam-vals-is]] fired here — when a later round gives the
opposite fault to an earlier one, stop correcting and look at what I am requiring.

**How to apply.** When a safety rule turns on whether the other person is *allowed*:

1. **Do not write a fourth credential.** Name the dead end in the spec so the next round
   does not spend itself on one either.
2. **Give the child a test on the SITUATION, not on the adult.** The tests that survived
   all six rounds ask nothing about the adult's standing: such care is never a secret;
   the reason given does not make it right, and neither does who he is; she says it
   whatever reason was given, to the next adult if the first does not listen.
3. **A protection the child may invoke is a right, not a validity test.** "Someone she
   trusts may be there" is hers to ask for. The moment it becomes what *makes the care
   legitimate*, an abuser can satisfy it.
4. **The exception describes what care exists; it is not a checklist she applies.**

Two smaller faults from the same pass, both the shape of
[[n-waarskuwing-moet-elke-broer-dek]]: a closed list ("only … nobody else") used the
feminine form of the nursing word, so a male nurse giving lawful care fell outside a rule
the lesson shuts absolutely; and the what-counts-as-a-yes block listed only what was
*never* a yes, so a yes once given read as standing. Related:
[[veiligheidslesse-lewer-die-ergste-foute]], [[elke-sin-waar-die-prentjie-vals]].
