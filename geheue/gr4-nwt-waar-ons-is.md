also gives the word **stut** its own lesson before the frame-structure lesson leans on it.
Everything after shifts by one: Term 3 starts at 18, and the year runs to about 31.

**14 and 15 are DONE and approved** — "'n Vel papier wat 'n boek dra" (568) and
"Van plat vel tot sterk buis" (575). Both took five to six revision rounds, far more
than any Term 1 lesson, and the reasons are recorded in
[[n-regstelling-ontwrig-sy-bure]] and [[spesifikasies-word-nooit-nagegaan]]: the two
lessons share a mechanism, and most of the later findings came from my own revision
briefs rather than from the drafts.

Two non-faults left for the downstream human pass, both flagged by checkers and both
deliberately not fixed: "Sy vel" in lesson 14 reads for four words as *his skin*
before "as die plat vel" resolves it, and one "sy" in the mechanism block means a
cornered pillar where an earlier "hy" in the same block meant the round one. Both
checked out as unambiguous; neither is worth another cycle.

**The book's Term 2 is pages 61-101, mapped by hand**, and its register runs HARDER than
our band: 1.62 syllables a word against our 1.52 ceiling, 15.7% long words against 13%.
The band does not move. Expect the writer to fight for it and the gate to catch failures
that look like nothing else we have seen.

**Four things flagged into the planning briefs:**
- Lesson 11 must be built on lesson 10's vocabulary — CAPS asks for the water cycle
  explained *in terms of change of state*, not told as the rain story.
- Lesson 13 must not touch metals and non-metals. Grade 5 content, sitting two pages away

- Lesson 16 overlaps Term 1's lesson 8, which already taught frame structures — and that
  lesson's shell definition cost a revision round once already.
- Lesson 17 is cultural content and needs verification outside CAPS. CAPS is not a
  language authority, and a people's own word for their own building is a different order
  of error. Also: CAPS writes "ledemaatbene is stutte", and **bene is forbidden for
  animals** in our house style.

**Every shared wording in Term 3 matches character for character** — `energie` across five

the extract started carrying `gedeelde_omskrywings`, and the first with no new drift in it.

**What Term 3 cost, and why.** Lesson 22 took four writer passes and lesson 24 took five. Almost
none of it was the writer: the faults were in the specification, in the harness, or in my own
briefs. See [[n-versagting-vat-die-algemene-helfte-saam]],
[[die-uittreksel-het-sy-eie-kruisverwysings-verloor]],
[[moenie-in-die-spek-skryf-wat-jy-nie-nagegaan-het-nie]] and
[[n-bewering-oor-afrikaanse-taalgebruik-is-nie-n-vereiste-nie]]. The recurring shape is a
correction made where it was noticed and left standing in the two or three other places the same
requirement was written — it happened to the ruler press, the rubber band's rule, the seam
sentence, and the Afrikaans phrase.

**Three rulings from this term worth carrying forward:**
- **The deafness decision.** Across lessons 23 to 25 a deaf or hard-of-hearing child must not read
  this work as a warning about herself. One mandatory sentence in 25, a vibration-feeling sentence
  in 23, and lesson 25's arc pointed at protecting your own hearing. Both checkers judged the
  placement right rather than merely acceptable.
- **A ruler cannot bob visibly and sound a note at the same overhang.** The visible range is
  inaudible and the audible range is invisible. Lesson 24 rests on hearing; what a learner sees is
  the blurred band widening.
- **Afrikaans says "harder", not "hoër".** Drico settled it. A whole block and six specification
  fields were built on the English problem.

## Still open, subject-wide

appear in more than one lesson: *eienskap, geraamte, habitat, materiaal, ru-olie, struktuur, stut,
waterdamp*. `bin/woordelysdrif.py` reports them. Four are cheap — the odd one out is an
undelivered lesson. Three touch delivered lessons and would need errata.

**The decision that unlocks the rest: may a shared definition change its examples per lesson?**
Two delivered lessons carry the same wording for *materiaal* with different examples, each fitted
to its own lesson. If that is allowed, *materiaal* stops being drift and *geraamte* probably
follows.
