1 October 2026, Grade 7 LO. In Self 1 I recorded that the helpful item's *"without
anyone asking"* was **not** a fault — explicitly "so a later round does not start on
it" — and gave my reasons: the word means *willing* to help, and the lesson's own
broader wording two blocks earlier still governs. A fifth fact check then showed those
are the reasons *against* the item: if the broader test governs, the list excludes a
learner who passes it, and the lesson contradicts itself. Same day, Self 7: I recorded
the international-test comparison as "strictly relative and therefore true". Without
naming who the comparison is with, "the boys" reads as the boys of those many
countries, and that reading is false.

A third instance the same day, softer: I promoted a sentence to a protected
requirement because a fact check called it the strongest claim in the lesson. The next
fact check, searching it separately, split it in two — the claim was sound, the
majority quantifier was not. My protection was too wide rather than wrong.

**Why:** an ordinary wrong requirement gets caught by the next round. A note that
declares a matter *closed* turns the next round away — it is the one shape that
survives the thing designed to catch it. And my reasons for closing it are written in
the register of a finding, so a checker reads them as established.

**How to apply:** do not write "this is not a fault" into a spec unless a checker
found it and cleared it, and then say which checker and what it tested. Record the
OBSERVATION without the verdict: "the helpful item rests on unasked help" is useful to
the next round; "and that is not a fault" is not mine to add. When protecting a
sentence a checker confirmed, protect the specific claim it confirmed, not the whole
sentence — a quantifier inside a confirmed claim is a separate question. Related:
