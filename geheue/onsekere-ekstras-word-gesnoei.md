Standing rule from Drico, 2026-08-19: if a detail is **not in CAPS and not asked for
by the spec**, and the fact check comes back `onseker` or `weerspreek` on it, **cut it
and report afterwards.** Do not escalate, do not spend rounds refining it.

Escalate instead when the uncertainty touches something the spec requires — a `kern`
item, or an `aanvulling` the planner justified — because a curriculum requirement
cannot be deleted to make a problem go away. Also escalate if cutting would leave the
lesson thin or a concept half-explained: that is a trade-off, not a tidy-up.

**Why:** unrequested colour cannot help coverage, because no requirement needs it,
but it can be wrong. A wet-feet detail nobody asked for cost four checking rounds on
a lesson about what a raft is made of. Drico's framing: "if there are unsurity and
its just something extra not in caps, then lets not waste time."

**Why this judgement lands on the orchestrator and not on a checker:** the fact
checker deliberately never sees the spec, so it cannot tell required content from
unrequested colour, and it must keep escalating every uncertainty. Only the
orchestrator holds both the report and the spec, so only the orchestrator can apply
this rule.

**Widened on 2026-08-19, in Drico's words:** "If such an unsure moment comes up
again, feel free to rather leave it out (if it doesn't do harm to the content
quality)." So the rule is no longer only about unrequested content — it covers any
detail that cannot be settled, including a word choice. Leave it out unless taking it
out costs the lesson something real. Only then does it become a decision.

**The feedback loop that makes it safe:** Drico will judge from the three test
lessons whether cutting has made them too thin, and say so. Cut-first is provisional
on that. Related: [[wanneer-vra-en-wanneer-doen]],
