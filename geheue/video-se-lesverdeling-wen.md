Drico, 31 August 2026, after I showed him that the ten Gr 4 SW Geskiedenis videos divide
"Vervoer oor tyd heen" differently from CAPS's thirteen items: *"Lets merge the caps topics
such that it matches the video's when we are doing this backwards process. We treat it as
one lesson, therefore the volume should be suitable for one lesson as well. These video's
are created by a teacher basd on how they teach the topics, which is useful actually."*

I had recommended the opposite — text on CAPS, two texts sharing one video — and argued a
learner examined on Chinese junks should find a section about ships rather than one that
also does rafts. He overruled it, and his reason is better than mine: **a teacher's own
division of a topic is evidence about how the topic teaches.** A CAPS bullet list is not.

**Why:** the merges are not arbitrary. Carts and the bicycle go together because both are
"the wheel arrives"; rafts and the first sailing ships go together because both are "before
engines". That is pedagogy, and nothing in the CAPS document carries it.

**How to apply.**

* **Coverage does not loosen.** Every bullet is still covered in full, and every item CAPS
  names explicitly is still mandatory. Only the seams move — coverage from CAPS, division
  from the teacher.
* **One lesson's budget, not two.** Divide the sub-topic's measured volume by the new,
  smaller lesson count. Merging spends length rather than buying it, so **the merged lesson
  is the tight one** — plan its item-count division before writing rather than finding out
  in a revision. Land's merged lesson owes a cart, a wagon, a coach and three generations of
  bicycle in 464 words.
* This applies **only where a video already exists**, which today means Gr 4 Sosiale
  Wetenskappe. Everywhere else the video follows the text and one lesson is one CAPS bullet.

He also dropped the exhaust-fumes case study outright — *"Dont worry about that gevallestudy
at all"* — which CAPS lists with its own hour and which had no video.
