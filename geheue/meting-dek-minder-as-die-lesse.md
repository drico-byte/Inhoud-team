Decided 2026-08-24, on Gr 4 NWT Term 2, and Drico approved the resulting numbers.

## The trap

The normal budget is measured textbook volume divided by lesson count. That is only
sound when the measurement covers **every** lesson in the sub-topic. Twice in Term 2
it did not, because CAPS requires content the Platinum Gr 4 book simply omits:

- **Materiaal om ons** — 1382 words measured, covering two of three lessons. No water
  cycle anywhere in the book, and no evaporation or condensation either.
- **Sterk raamstrukture** — 443 words measured, covering one of two. No indigenous
  structures at all: not Zulu, Xhosa or Nama, not one house name.

Dividing evenly would have cut the covered lessons **below what the book itself spends
on them**, to buy length for a lesson the measurement never saw. Drico's phrase for it
after I explained: like three people splitting a bill when only two ordered.

## The rule

Count how many of the sub-topic's lessons the measurement actually covers. If that is
fewer than the lesson count, the sub-topic uses `begroting_basis: "vereistes"`: the
covered lessons keep the book's own per-lesson volume, and the uncovered ones are sized
from what CAPS requires. Both need the reasoning written down.

**Consequence worth expecting:** the term then exceeds the book. Term 2 comes to roughly
5050 words against the book's 4270 — about a fifth more, almost entirely those two
lessons. That is the right direction and the first time we have not been at parity.

Related: [[skrywer-oorlewer-nie-onderlewer-nie]], [[kruistoets-die-termynplan-teen-kaps]],
[[wiskunde-video-eerste]] (which was the first use of a requirement-based budget).

## And the mapping problem underneath it

None of this was visible until the pages were mapped by hand. The book runs Term 2 in a
different order from CAPS and shares **not one section title** with it, so heading
hunting found nothing usable. The profiler now accepts explicit page ranges per
sub-topic, several each, which also lets content be kept off the measurement — two pages
of metals and non-metals, which is Grade 5, would otherwise have bought space in a Grade
4 lesson. See [[moenie-op-voorkoms-sorteer-nie]] for the general shape: measure on what a
thing is, not on what it looks like from outside.
