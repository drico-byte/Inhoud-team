Decided 2026-08-28. Drico: "no if we can get about 400-500 then thats good."

**Why there is no measurement.** Heading matching could not bound this book's Term 4.
Three runs gave three answers; ten of thirteen CAPS-derived labels found nothing; only
two boundaries were reproducible (something starts at p142, something at p166). The
stored profile had claimed 59 pages and 11 568 words for the term — against 15–22 pages
and 2 436–4 266 words for every other term in the same book — because the last section
ran without an end marker to the final page and swallowed the back matter. That figure
was rejected, not divided.

**What the budget rests on instead.** The book's own per-lesson volume across the three
terms that *were* mapped: 10 405 words over 24 lessons = **434 words a lesson** (Term 1
304, Term 2 474, Term 3 529). So this is still parity with the book — just measured
across the year rather than inside one unbounded topic. It is not a guess, and it is not
taste, which matters because [[spesifikasies-word-nooit-nagegaan]].

**How it is recorded.** `begroting_basis: "vereistes"` in the spec, which switches off
even division and stops `onderwerp_woorde` being treated as the anchor. The spec needs a
`begroting_basis_nota`, and every lesson needs its own `begrotingsnota` saying how its
number was derived. Under this basis, sizing by *requirement count* is correct; sizing
by judged importance is still not — see [[meting-dek-minder-as-die-lesse]], which is the
same basis used for the same kind of reason.

**Still open, and cheap to close.** If anyone maps the pages by hand from the headings
worksheet, fill the ranges into the Term 4 labels file and re-measure at 300 dpi. The
reasoning is written into that file so the phantom 11 568 cannot come back.
