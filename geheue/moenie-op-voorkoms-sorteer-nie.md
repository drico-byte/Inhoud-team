Learned the expensive way on 2026-08-19: three revision rounds on one intuition block,
all spent trying to find a visual test that separates a bone from a pebble.

Each attempt was corrected and each correction opened a new hole. "You can see which
one lived" failed because a bone is recognisable and a pebble is not. Naming the knobs,
the hollow shaft and the spongy ends failed for ribs and chops. "The pebble has no such
holes" failed for scoria. "A hard outer layer around a different inside" failed for
geodes, banded agate and flint, which all have exactly that. "Solid the whole way
through" failed for scoria and geodes.

**There is no visual test. The pattern of failures was the answer and it took three
rounds to hear it.**

**The rule: do not rest a classification on appearance.** Rest it on something that has
no exceptions. For living, dead and never-alive that is *provenance and capability* —
where did the thing come from, and can it still feed itself or grow. Those two questions
sort a dry bean, a dead leaf, a bone, a stone and a living dog correctly, and they were
already in the lesson while three rounds were spent on the interior of a rock.

If a visual contrast is genuinely wanted, use the **regularity** of the pattern rather
than its content: every chicken drumstick looks the same inside, while every pebble looks
different inside. That is true, it is a real distinction between biological and
geological structure, and it has no counterexample.

**How to spot it early:** if a claim takes the form "X looks like this and Y does not",
ask what object a Grade 4 might be holding that breaks it. If the answer takes more than a
moment, the test is the wrong kind. Related: [[onsekere-ekstras-word-gesnoei]]
