I told a planner that plants making their own food was new ground for this
sub-topic, because the Term 1 lesson on how a plant grows never says it. That
lesson really doesn't. But two other already-delivered Term 1 lessons do — a green
plant makes its own food in its leaves with sunlight — and the approved glossary
entry for *voeding* carries the food-making half as well.

**Why it went wrong:** I searched three lessons I had picked by name, and inside
them I searched only the glossary terms, not the study text. Three files chosen by
guess is not a search of the subject. The planner caught it and fixed it at source.

**What it would have cost if it hadn't:** the lesson would have spent budget
teaching approved content again, and a fresh formulation would have become a third
competing wording of a claim two delivered lessons already make — the same drift
the glossary check exists to catch, arriving through the front door.

**How to apply:** before telling any agent that something is new, absent, or
already covered, search the full text of every delivered lesson in the subject, not
a shortlist and not only the glossary. State it as a finding with the lessons
named, so the agent can check me. Same shape as the partial sweeps in
[[spesifikasies-word-nooit-nagegaan]]: a search that stops early reads exactly like
a search that found nothing.
