**Gr 4 Geography, settlements lesson 7, 29 September 2026.** The same draft, checked
twice. Round one: 27 items, one contradiction. Round two: 34 items, **five**
contradictions — in a draft that had already been corrected once.

The difference was one instruction in the brief. Round two said: *the lesson has four
blocks describing a farm, a village, a town and a city, in that order — read them as a
set and ask what a learner concludes from the ordering and from what each block does and
does not mention.*

What that found, none of which is a false sentence:

* The blocks run *some houses* → *most houses* → *the houses*, so the city is the only
  place in the lesson where nobody carries water. South Africa's worst on-site water
  access is in fact urban — the informal settlements concentrated in the metros.
* Town-then-city implies city water is more reliable. The country's worst record for
  long interruptions belongs to one of its largest cities; another large city has one of
  the best. Reliability follows maintenance, not size.
* Growing your own food was given to the farm and denied to the village. Household food
  growing sits mostly *outside* the metros, and workers on commercial farms buy their
  food and are among the most food-insecure people in the country.

**Why sentence-level checking cannot see these.** Each block is true alone. The claim
lives in what one item has that its siblings do not, and in the order they stand in. A
checker reading down a list has no reason to hold four blocks in mind at once — so it
will not, unless asked.

**How to apply.** Whenever a lesson contains a parallel structure — a set of places, a
set of creatures, a set of periods, a before-and-after — put it in the fact brief
explicitly: *read these as a set, and say what the ordering and the omissions teach.*
Name the structure, because the checker cannot tell which blocks are meant to be
parallel. And ask for the **direction** of any implication, not just its presence.

This is the same blindness as [[n-waarskuwing-moet-elke-broer-dek]] (a caution covering
some siblings and not others) and the third costume in
[[n-regstelde-fout-kom-in-n-ander-gedaante-terug]] (the same false picture delivered by
arrangement after the false sentence was removed). All three are list-shaped, and all
three are invisible to a reader who takes one item at a time.

**And the cost of not doing it is a wasted round**, not a missed fault — the second check
found these on text that had already been revised once, so every correction from round
one had to be re-verified against them. Ask in the first brief.
