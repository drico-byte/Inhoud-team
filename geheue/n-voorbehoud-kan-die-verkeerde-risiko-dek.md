A lesson claimed Mapungubwe was Southern Africa's first **known** town, and the ruling
making "known" mandatory gave its own reason: that it was the smallest change making the
sentence true under both camps of the literature. A fact check showed it is not. "Known"
guards against an undiscovered earlier town — but every real competitor was already known
(Mapela Hill, dated about two centuries earlier and called larger; K2, with 1 000–2 000
people and treated alongside Mapungubwe as the region's first urban centres; Toutswemogala;
Bosutswe). So the hedge excluded none of them. Worse, by the lesson's own definition of a
town, K2 qualified — the claim failed its own test.

**Two things to apply.**

A hedge must be checked against the actual competitors, not against the abstract
possibility it is phrased to cover. Ask which specific rivals the qualifier excludes; if the
answer is none, the qualifier is decoration that makes the claim look settled, which is
worse than no qualifier at all.

And a ruling that states its own basis can have that basis refuted without the ruling being
touched. That is an escalation, not a correction I may apply — even when the same person
later made the matching narrowing on a sibling claim, and even when the evidence is the
same evidence. I nearly carried that sibling ruling across; the field itself said in
capitals to keep the two claims apart, deliberately, which I only saw because I read the
field before writing. Related: [[wanneer-vra-en-wanneer-doen]],
