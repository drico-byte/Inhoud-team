29 September 2026, Grade 7 LO, Werk 2. One field ordered the memory-aid entry to stay
**wide** (so it would cover chunking) and ordered its second sentence — *the aid gives no
understanding by itself* — to stay **word for word**. Both orders were written for good
reasons, months of rounds apart, and each is defensible alone.

Together they are false. Read as widely as the first order demands, the definition also
covers a diagram, a summary or an explanation in your own words, and those *do* build
understanding. So the absolute is false of the category as worded.

**This is not two fields contradicting each other** — see [[kern-en-feiterisiko-weerspreek-mekaar]]
for that shape, where one field orders what another forbids. Here nothing contradicts:
both can be obeyed, and obeying both produces a false lesson. No sweep finds it, because
there is no collision to detect. A fact checker found it by reading the entry alone, which
is the only way it shows up — [[n-begrip-word-alleen-gelees]].

**The fix is neither half.** Dropping the breadth loses chunking; dropping the absolute
loses the point of the entry. What was wrong was the *axis* the definition was drawn on:
it named the FORM (an easily remembered shape you make of the work), and every counterexample
is also a form. Bound it instead by what the aid **does** — makes a bare list or an order
easier to recall — and chunking stays in while a summary falls out, and the absolute becomes
true of everything left. The study text already had that narrower form; only the entry
lagged.

**How to spot it:** when two dated orders about one sentence pull in opposite directions and
both are defensible, don't pick a winner and don't average them. Ask what each one is
actually protecting, then look for a boundary that gives both — usually a different axis
from the one the definition is currently drawn on. If no such boundary exists, *then* it is
a decision for a person.
