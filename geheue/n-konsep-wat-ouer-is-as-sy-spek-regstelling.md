14 October 2026, Gr 6 SW. A withdrawal sweep over all 73 drafts turned up two drafts carrying
wording their own spec forbids **by name**, with the prohibition dated and reasoned:

* Ontdekkingsreisigers 3 said Galileo's work falls "naby die einde van die Renaissance". The
  spec forbids exactly that phrase, names it as the delivered draft's own form, and gives the
  reason: his work is 17th century, outside the two centuries this sub-topic teaches.
* Ontdekkingsreisigers 9 said the sailors were "maande lank weg van die huis". The spec orders
  **jare** and forbids the months form, because the round trip plus the Asian contract came to
  roughly four and a half years.

**Why they survived: a correction to a spec does not touch a draft, and nothing re-gates a
gated lesson.** Both specs were corrected on 8–9 October; both drafts were written on
29 September and have sat gated ever since. The fault would have surfaced only when a fact
checker finally read the lesson — at the cost of a full check plus a writer round, per lesson.

**The triage that finds them cheaply:** for every draft, compare when the draft last changed
against the newest dated record anywhere in its spec entry. On Gr 6 SW that flagged 20 of 73.
It is a reading list, not a fault list, and it has two false-positive classes worth knowing:

* **content dates.** A lesson about an election quotes "4 November 2026", and a date regex
  reads it as a record newer than everything. Date the RECORD, not every date in the field.
* **records that document rather than correct.** Three delivered Demokrasie lessons carry
  records dated after delivery that say the delivered wording was kept deliberately. Nothing
  is behind; the record exists to stop someone "fixing" it. See
  [[n-later-nasien-is-nie-n-newer-bron-nie]].

**So the rule: when a sub-topic's drafts all predate its spec corrections, run a writer pass
over the sub-topic before any checker reads it.** One briefed round that carries every
withdrawal at once is far cheaper than discovering them one per fact check — which is the
same lesson as [[n-regstelde-fout-kom-in-n-ander-gedaante-terug]], one level up. Related:
[[die-spek-was-die-fout-sewentien-keer]], [[n-teruggetrekte-beslissing-bly-in-hoofletters-staan]],
[[roep-die-hardloper-na-n-spekwysiging]].
