Seen twice in one sitting on Gr 4 NWT Term 4, 2026-08-28, and it will recur.

**The shape.** The fact checker finds that a block lets a learner reason on to something
false, and requires a qualifier to close it. The writer adds it. The coverage checker
then lists that same qualifier under `oortollig`, because no `kern` item, no
`aanvulling` and no flagged concept asks for it — which is true, and which is exactly
what that checker is for.

**Two live examples.**

- *Die Son.* The car-lights analogy let a child conclude that dim stars are the far
  ones. The fix was two sentences saying stars differ in how much light they give off.
  Coverage flagged both as unrequested.
- *Die Maan, les 1.* The apparent-size claim pinned the match on distance alone, which
  is false — something 400 times farther looks smaller. The fix ties the width ratio and
  the distance ratio together. Coverage flagged the width ratio and the general rule
  about distance as surplus.

**The ruling: keep the caveat, and do not send it back.** Cutting reinstates an error a
sourced check already caught. Coverage is right that nothing asked for it and right to
record it; its own prompt says `oortollig` is an observation and never changes a verdict.
The two checkers are answering different questions and neither is wrong.

**Why it happens, and why it is not a bug.** The spec is written before the draft, so it
cannot know which sentence will invite a false inference. Only a sourced check on real
prose finds that. So a correct lesson ends up slightly over-supplied against its spec,
and that is the cheaper failure. Compare [[verklaar-moenie-net-dek-nie]]: covering a
requirement and teaching something false is the expensive one.

**What to do with it.** Note it and move on. Where the caveat is large enough to matter
against the budget, the fix belongs in the spec at the next revision, not in the draft —
see [[moenie-n-regstelling-verder-vat-as-die-bevinding-nie]] for why the draft is the
wrong place to settle it. Related: [[n-regstelling-ontwrig-sy-bure]].

## Three rounds on one quantifier, two checks contradicting each other, and the answer was to say nothing

**8 October 2026.** One sentence of a glossary entry defining neglect went through three
forms in two days, and **every one was faulted by a check**:

1. *one **serious** failure is enough* — contradicted: no statutory definition carries a
   severity measure, and the form excluded the child whose carer's lapses are many but
   each small, which is the commonest case.
2. the severity word removed — a check then found the entry **wider than the law**,
   because the statute's own bound (the child's *basic* needs) had never been there.
3. the bound restored, *one lapse is enough* left standing — **contradicted outright**: no
   source supports it, and three point the other way.

**So two fact checks gave opposite advice about how to bound the same claim.** The first
said a severity word has no support; the third's own remedy was to reintroduce a
continuing-failure or severity framing. Each was right about what it read.

**The resolution: the statute has no frequency element either way.** It defines neglect as
a failure to provide for a child's basic needs and says nothing about how many times. So
**silence is the only position neither check contradicts** — it asserts nothing
unsupported, and it does not exclude the child whose neglect happened once, because
**silence is not exclusion**. The frequency sentence came out entirely and the bound does
the work alone.

**The general rule, which is what to take from this:**

> **When two checks disagree about how to bound a claim, stop choosing between their
> bounds and go and ask whether the source bounds it at all.** If it does not, write
> nothing about that dimension. Every wording you pick there states a rule no authority
> states, and each one will be contradicted by whichever check reads the other half of
> the literature.

**Two riders.**

* **Do not apply the fix to the siblings.** The same formula sat in two other entries of
  that lesson and is **correct there**, because behind a single deliberate *act* the
  statute's own definition makes one occasion sufficient. Behind a *failure to provide* it
  does not. One formula could not carry all three, and "fixing" all three together is how
  the fault arose — [[moenie-n-regstelling-verder-vat-as-die-bevinding-nie]].
* **Record the whole history in one line in the draft's note**, not just the current form.
  Three faulted wordings and the reason for the silence is what stops a fourth writer
  "restoring" a quantifier and starting the cycle again.

Related: [[n-regstelling-ontwrig-sy-bure]] (a wrong qualifier can be holding the place of
the right one — the same sentence, one step earlier),
[[drie-maniere-om-n-bewering-reg-te-maak]], [[onsekere-ekstras-word-gesnoei]].

## Contradicted is not untestable, and the same sentence gets opposite treatment

**8 October 2026.** Two glossary entries in one lesson ended with the **identical**
sentence — *once is enough*. I removed it from one and kept it in the other, deliberately,
and the reason is the rule:

* At **`verwaarlosing`** the claim was **contradicted**: three sources pointed the other
  way. A contradicted claim goes — and where the source is silent on the dimension
  altogether, what replaces it is **silence**, not a fourth wording.
* At **`mishandeling`** the claim is **untestable**: the word is defined nowhere in South
  African law, so no authority addresses the question at all. Nothing contradicts it.
  **It stays**, because removing a protective sentence for no verifiable gain costs
  something real, and a child reading that entry alone loses the reassurance.

**So "onseker" splits, and the split decides the action:**

| what the check found | what to do |
|---|---|
| sources contradict the claim | remove it; if the source is silent on that dimension, say nothing |
| sources conflict with each other | keep the best-supported reading, disclose briefly |
| sources are thin | keep if nothing points the other way; record it |
| **no authority exists** | **keep if it is protective and nothing contradicts it** |
| no source was reached | re-run the check; this is not a verdict |

**The trap this avoids** is making the three entries consistent with each other, which is
what produced the fault in the first place and which the requirement now forbids by name.
**Consistency between siblings is not a goal** — each entry answers to its own word and
its own authority, and two of these four words have statutory definitions while two are
constitutional words left to the courts. Identical sentences in different entries can be
right, wrong and unanswerable respectively.
