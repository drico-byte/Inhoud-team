Adopted 2026-08-19 after measuring my own record on lesson 1's intuition block.

**Three separate factual errors in that block came from sentences I wrote by hand**
rather than from the writer:

1. "the wood comes back up *because* your hand pushed it down" — causation reversed.
2. "too much cargo makes the raft heavier than all the water it can push aside" —
   true but so abstract a nine-year-old could not follow it, which the reviewer
   caught and no check could.
3. "if the load is heavier than that water, the raft goes under" — weighed the cargo
   alone and forgot the water must hold the raft up too, promising several times the
   real capacity.

Each was a small, well-specified edit that looked safe. Each introduced a defect the
fact checker then had to find.

**Rule: content changes go through the writer, even one-sentence ones.** Give it the
correction, the verified constraints and the length, and let it write. I orchestrate,
measure, route and decide — I do not compose lesson text.

**Why:** the writer has the standard loaded, the register bands in view and the voice
calibration; I have none of that when I patch a sentence in place. And a hand edit
skips the one discipline the pipeline depends on — that content is produced by a
component whose prompt is versioned and whose output is recorded in provenance.

**The exception, kept narrow:** mechanical non-prose edits — deleting an orphaned
glossary entry, pruning a wordlist, correcting a provenance note. Anything a learner
reads goes to the writer.
