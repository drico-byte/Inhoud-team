**Gr 4 Geography, first three lessons, 29 September 2026. Eight faults; five were in
the specs, and every one of those five came from the video.**

The chain is always the same. The video says it. The planner, told to use the video's
words, carries it into `kern`. Whoever settles the shared wordings copies `kern`'s
phrasing into the agreed scope. The writer, reading both, writes it. **One phrase, four
places** — and the fourth is the one a later lesson copies verbatim.

What it produced: a settlement defined as where people live *and work* (no reference
makes work a test, and the lesson contradicted itself four blocks later with commuters);
a need defined as what you need to live *comfortably* (comfort is exactly what separates
a want from a need); an animal sheltered in a *skuur* or a *kraal* (a skuur stores the
harvest, a kraal is an open enclosure against predators — neither keeps weather off);
a factory defined as a *large* building; and crops put before livestock on a farm, which
reverses South African farmland.

**How to apply.** When a fact check contradicts a claim in a video-first subject, do not
fix the draft. Find which of the four places the phrase entered, and **sweep the claim
across all of them** — the seam, every `kern` item, the shared wordings, and every other
field that repeats it. Sweeping the CLAIM rather than the field a checker named found two
more instances of one correction here, and the second was the *next lesson's own
requirement*, which would have reinstated the withdrawn word in the one place that
mattered most. See [[vee-die-bewering-oor-al-die-spesifikasies]] and
[[n-spek-se-dieselfde-ding-in-twee-velde]].

**And put a false video claim in the list a writer actually reads.** Twice in one day a
video claim was correctly recorded in `feiterisiko` and absent from the seam's
`moenie_hierdie_bewerings_herhaal_nie`. That list is the one a writer consults to know
what the video says that the text must not repeat; a caution filed anywhere else arrives
at the wrong moment. Both survived only because the writer happened to read both lists,
and one writer said so explicitly — a faster one would have met the claim only where
nothing flagged it.

**The writers refusing a bad instruction is not a safeguard.** Six agents in a row
refused a wrong profiler config the runner printed in the very line they are told to copy
verbatim. Every one was right, and that is exactly the wrong thing to rely on: it only
takes one that does not. Fix the instruction, not the habit of catching it.
