**Gr 4 Geography, first three lessons, 29 September 2026. Eight faults; five were in
the specs, and every one of those five came from the video.**

The chain is always the same. The video says it. The planner, told to use the video's
words, carries it into `kern`. Whoever settles the shared wordings copies `kern`'s
phrasing into the agreed scope. The writer, reading both, writes it. **One phrase, four
places** — and the fourth is the one a later lesson copies verbatim.

What it produced: a settlement defined as where people live *and work* (no reference
makes work a test, and the lesson contradicted itself four blocks later with commuters);
a need defined as what you need to live *comfortably* (comfort is exactly what separates
a want from a need); an animal sheltered in a *skuur* or a *kraal* (a skuur stores the
harvest, a kraal is an open enclosure against predators — neither keeps weather off);
a factory defined as a *large* building; and crops put before livestock on a farm, which
reverses South African farmland.

**How to apply.** When a fact check contradicts a claim in a video-first subject, do not
fix the draft. Find which of the four places the phrase entered, and **sweep the claim
across all of them** — the seam, every `kern` item, the shared wordings, and every other
field that repeats it. Sweeping the CLAIM rather than the field a checker named found two
more instances of one correction here, and the second was the *next lesson's own
requirement*, which would have reinstated the withdrawn word in the one place that
mattered most. See [[vee-die-bewering-oor-al-die-spesifikasies]] and
[[n-spek-se-dieselfde-ding-in-twee-velde]].

**And put a false video claim in the list a writer actually reads.** Twice in one day a
video claim was correctly recorded in `feiterisiko` and absent from the seam's
`moenie_hierdie_bewerings_herhaal_nie`. That list is the one a writer consults to know
what the video says that the text must not repeat; a caution filed anywhere else arrives
at the wrong moment. Both survived only because the writer happened to read both lists,
and one writer said so explicitly — a faster one would have met the claim only where
nothing flagged it.

**The writers refusing a bad instruction is not a safeguard.** Six agents in a row
refused a wrong profiler config the runner printed in the very line they are told to copy
verbatim. Every one was right, and that is exactly the wrong thing to rely on: it only
takes one that does not. Fix the instruction, not the habit of catching it.

**Addendum, same day: sweep on the WITHDRAWN WORDING, not on the ruling that withdrew it.**
When I reversed the *skuiling* decision I swept for the phrases that carried my ruling
("DIE OMSKRYWING BLY BY MENSE") and found none left. But the withdrawn DEFINITION was
still sitting in two other fields, quoted as settled — including lesson 6's own
requirement, which orders that lesson to copy lesson 3's sentence **word for word** and
then quoted the old sentence. That is the field that would actually have reinstated it.

A coverage checker found one of the two. Sweeping on the wording found both. So after any
reversal, run two sweeps: one for the ruling's own words, and one for the **text of the
thing withdrawn**.

And note which field keeps lagging: the spec's expected-glossary note has now been the
laggard three times, because its whole job is to restate scopes that live somewhere else.
A field whose content is a copy will always lag. Related:
[[n-spek-se-dieselfde-ding-in-twee-velde]], [[n-teruggetrekte-beslissing-bly-in-hoofletters-staan]].

**29 September 2026, second addendum: I put the error into the seam MYSELF, then
corrected it everywhere except there.**

The note above is about a wrong phrase arriving from a video and spreading. This is the
same shape with the opposite origin, and it is worse, because the spreading was my doing.

I widened a sentence — the names on a globe stand nowhere on the real Earth became *the
names and borders* — and I wrote that widening into the seam's
`wat_in_die_plek_daarvan`, the field that tells a writer what replaces a killed video
claim. Hours later a fact check killed the widening: borders really are marked on the
ground, and South Africa's own are. I withdrew it in the requirement item and **did not
sweep the seam**.

So the spec simultaneously forbade the widening in one field and **ordered it** in
another. A coverage checker found it and made the point that settles how seriously to
take this class: *the previous coverage report had cited that exact seam field as the
live requirement, and approved the borders sentence against it.* The wrong field was
already being used as the authority.

**How to apply.**

* **A seam entry is an ORDER, not a note.** `wat_in_die_plek_daarvan` tells the writer
  what to write. When a ruling changes, that field is as binding as `kern` and has to
  move with it — and it lives in `kaps/lesindeks/…videonate.json`, so the fix goes there
  first and re-syncs, or it is overwritten.
* **Sweep on what you WROTE, not only on what a checker named.** The fields I had edited
  that day were the ones to re-read, and I re-read the one the finding pointed at.
* **A correction's own reasoning can assert the refuted claim.** Two dated paragraphs
  explained the fix by listing "coloured countries, borders and printed names, none of
  which exists on the real Earth" — the very absolute that had just been contradicted,
  sitting above the correction in a field read top to bottom. Reasoning needs the sweep
  too.
