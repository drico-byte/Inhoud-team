## What CAPS already settles — do not re-derive this

**Budgets come from CAPS, not from the profiler.** Section 3.2.3 and 3.2.4 of the Afrikaans Huistaal
Gr 4-6 document prescribe text lengths outright. Grade 4: reading comprehension text 150-200 words;
longer listening text (story, drama, news) 150-200 words or up to 5 minutes; short story or folk
tale a learner writes 120-140 words; paragraph 50-60 words in 5-6 sentences; essay 100-120 words;
shorter texts 30-40 words. Grade 5 and 6 columns are in the same tables. **So the profiler is not
needed for this subject at all**, and the textbook rights exposure disappears with it.

**Language structures must be taught IN CONTEXT.** Section 3.3.5 is explicit: the content for
language structures is found under Listening & speaking, Reading & viewing, and Writing &
presenting; activities must relate to the texts studied in that two-week cycle; "alle taalstrukture
moet in konteks aangeleer word". It also notes there IS dedicated time for formal teaching of
language structures, so explanatory text is not off-model either. **Drico's own instinct — that the
four skills should be connected in theme every time — is CAPS's design, not a preference.**

**No specific story is prescribed.** Section 3.3.3: the text *type* is fixed per cycle, but "in die
meeste gevalle is geen spesifieke storie voorgeskryf nie". So we write our own texts, which
sidesteps the copyright problem entirely — we neither need nor may use the textbook's stories.

## The design that follows, sketched but NOT approved

One two-week cycle as one lesson, built around an original core text of the type CAPS prescribes,
with that cycle's language structures taught from it. The video may be the *listening* text (CAPS's
150-200 words or up to 5 min is a video), the written text the *reading* text, and the language work
drawn from the same story — covering three of the four skills in theme by construction, with only
writing happening off-platform. This would be the first time the video and the text have genuinely
different jobs rather than the text being a written version of the video.

**Machinery it would need:** a new block type for the core text, because it is the *object* of study
rather than explanation — its length comes from CAPS's table rather than the study budget, and a
story's sentence rhythm cannot be measured against the explanatory reading band unchanged. One
schema addition plus one measurement rule. No new skill.

## Questions worth taking to the specialists

1. Is one two-week cycle the right unit for one video plus one text, or is that too big?
2. Should the written text carry the same story as the video, or a different one of the same type?
3. Which language structures actually need formal explanation at Grade 4? CAPS says to choose
   carefully and "beperk dit tot 'n minimum".
4. Is an original story acceptable to teachers, or do they expect canonical texts?
5. Should the reading band for a narrative differ from the band for explanation?

**9 October 2026 — the specific blocker, so it is not rediscovered.** A fact check and then I
both tried to settle whether the figurative sense of `middelpunt` extends to a PLACE (a
centre of trade or activity) or only to a person or a matter. It decides whether "die
middelpunt van die hele vallei" is true at all: under the literal, geometric sense it is
false, because the places in question sit near one end of the valley rather than its middle.
Neither of us could reach an authority. WAT, HAT and Pharos are all behind subscription;
Afrikaans Wiktionary lists only the mathematical sense; the one reachable Afrikaans entry is
user-contributed and glosses the figurative sense as "persoon of saak", with "plek" appearing
only under the literal sense. Searching returns Dutch dictionaries, which are not evidence
about Afrikaans. **So do not try to settle a lexical question like this by search — it is a
question for the specialists, and the word must be protected with its reason in the meantime
so the outside check engages with it rather than quietly changing it.** Related:
[[n-bewering-oor-afrikaanse-taalgebruik-is-nie-n-vereiste-nie]],

**SETTLED 12 October 2026, by Lampies, and the way it was settled is the lesson.** Five fact
checks could not decide whether the figurative sense of `middelpunt` reaches a PLACE or only
a person or a matter. None of them failed to search: WAT, HAT and Pharos are all behind a
subscription, Afrikaans Wiktionary lists only the mathematical sense, and the one reachable
entry is user-contributed and gives "persoon of saak" without "plek". I then burned a search
of my own on it and added nothing.

Lampies answered it in one line as an Afrikaans speaker: the word is certainly not read only
in a mathematical sense. So the figurative sense covers a place, the entry and the lesson's
five uses are right, and the item was accepted by name in her name.

**The lesson for next time: ASK, EARLY.** A lexical question about Afrikaans is not something
more fact checks can settle, and five rounds of flagging it cost more than one question
would have. When an item comes back uncertain *because the sources are unreachable* rather
than because they conflict, and the question is about Afrikaans rather than about the world,
that is a question for the person, not for another checker. And when she asks to read the
text before deciding, show her the entry and every place the lesson uses the word — that is
what she needed to answer in one line. Related:
