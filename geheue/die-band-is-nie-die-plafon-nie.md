The gate reports two different limits and they are not the same number. The **volume band**
is the budget plus or minus 15 per cent, and it is what the PASS line is measured against.
The **ceiling** is the planned budget plus 12 per cent, and it is the binding figure. For a
270-word budget the band tops out at 310 while the ceiling is 302; for 500 the band reaches
575 and the ceiling is 560.

I read the band's upper bound off the gate output and briefed a repair agent that a lesson
at 298 words had about twelve to spare. It had four, and the agent checked the arithmetic
itself and told me — which turned optional trades into mandatory ones and changed what it
ordered.

**How to apply:** when telling anyone what headroom a lesson has, compute the ceiling from
the budget rather than quoting the band the gate prints beside it, and say which number it
is. Related: [[begroting-uit-kaps-ure]], [[n-plafon-kan-n-regstelling-onbekostigbaar-maak]],
[[my-soektogte-mis-en-dan-glo-ek-hulle]].

**And a third number: a LIST item counts toward volume but not toward register.** Read out of
the gate on 12 October 2026: list items are added to the word total, but only study and
reading prose enter the register measurement, so nothing in a `lys` can move mean sentence
length, syllables per word, the polysyllabic share or commas per sentence. A writer used this
deliberately, putting a required caveat inside a list item rather than a study block because
the lesson sat at 1.58 syllables against a hard-fail line of 1.611 — in prose the same
sentence would have joined the register sample for no gain. **So when something register-
sensitive has to be added to a lesson near its register limit, a list item is free of that
cost while still costing words.** Worth checking the same way before trusting any other
guess about which blocks a measure reads.
