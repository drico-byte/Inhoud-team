5 October 2026, Grade 7 LO, the lesson giving seven religions a block each. A writer added
to the Baha'i block: *"In this faith that written text carries the authority."* The phrase
**"in this faith"** was deliberate — it keeps the statement inside its own block so it is
not a comparison with any other religion.

**The coverage checker praised exactly that phrase for doing the containment work. The fact
checker named the same phrase as the fault.** Both read carefully; the fact checker is
right, because it tests against the world rather than against the spec.

**Why containment inverts in a set.** No other block says anything about where authority
sits. So a phrase that marks the claim as *this* member's own thereby marks it as **absent
from its siblings** — a learner takes it that the other six do not vest authority in their
scripture. That is false for at least three of them, and the lesson contradicted itself one
block earlier, where the Judaism block already says the Torah carries the teaching and the
law: the same claim, about a different religion.

Containment works when a block stands alone. **In a parallel set it manufactures a
contrast**, and the contrast is the thing nobody checked.

**How to apply.** When adding a sentence to one member of a parallel set, do not ask
"could this be read as a claim about the others?" — ask the sharper question: **does any
sibling say this, and would a reader conclude the silent ones deny it?** If the feature is
genuinely shared, either say it of the set or say it of nobody. If it is genuinely
distinctive, state the distinguishing fact itself rather than framing it as *this one's*
version of something general — for the Baha'i block, that only words written down and
authenticated count, and an unapproved account of what was said aloud does not.
