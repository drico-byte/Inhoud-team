Grade 5 dieregeraamtes, 10 September 2026. Three separate sentences placed a
bone the way it sits in a **standing human**, in a lesson that names a dog, a
snake, a fish and a lizard as its own examples:

* the vertebrae lying **"op mekaar"** — in a dog, a snake or a fish they lie
  **in a row**, one behind the other;
* the hole for the spinal cord **"onderaan"** the skull — in a four-legged
  animal it is at the **back**, and that difference is the standard test for
  reading two-leggedness off a skull;
* the hip bones **"laer af in die lyf"** — at the rear end of the backbone.

**Why:** this is not a word, it is a habit, so a keyword sweep never closes it —
it comes back in a different costume every round. The skull sentence alone moved
three times (a bowl → closed on all sides, also false → almost closed with a hole
underneath, still human-only). See
[[n-regstelde-fout-kom-in-n-ander-gedaante-terug]].

**How to apply:** take every sentence naming a place in the body — *onder, bo,
laer af, op mekaar, voor, agter* — and read it against a dog. If it falls over,
state the position **neutrally**: where one part joins the other, rather than
which way it points. Do not instead narrow the animal — the lesson has to keep
talking about animals in general. Related: [[n-versagting-vat-die-algemene-helfte-saam]],
[[n-begrip-word-alleen-gelees]].
