Drico, 8 September 2026, in his own words: *"its fine if a lesson in subject A
explains something that is in subject B. Repetition is good, and i think it is good
if learners see that its the same thing in 2 subjects. I dont want you to limit the
lesson creations to not have any repetition."*

## What prompted it

I had just found that the delivered Grade 4 science lesson **"Hard is nie hoog nie"**
already teaches, with a ruler, that a shorter length vibrates faster and sounds
higher — and says outright that a big drum's skin is bigger, vibrates more slowly,
and is why a big drum usually sounds lower.

The Grade 4 music lesson on high and low was about to teach the same thing. I wrote
into its spec that it **may not retell that physics** and must only use it.

**That instruction was wrong and has been lifted.** It treated overlap as waste.

## What binds instead

Only two things, and neither is about repetition:

1. **The agreed wording of a shared term stays word for word.** Repeat the content
   freely; do not restate the *definition* differently. Two subjects that define one
   word two ways teach two different things, and the end-year exam covers both. This
   is the one thing repetition genuinely can break. See
   [[n-begrip-word-alleen-gelees]] and the agreed-wordings rule in CLAUDE.md.
2. **The second subject may not contradict the first.** The science lesson says
   *"gewoonlik"* slower and *"gewoonlik"* lower. A music lesson may repeat the point
   but not harden the hedge into a rule.

Budget is a third consideration but it is a **judgement, not a prohibition**: every
sentence spent retelling is one not spent on the lesson's own work. The writer
weighs it.

## Why it is worth more than it looks

The two lessons here both fall in **Term 3**. A learner meets pitch in science and in
music in the same term. So the overlap is not redundancy at a distance — it is the
same idea arriving twice, from two directions, within weeks. That is a reason to
reference it **openly** rather than quietly avoid it.

Watch also for the shape [[energie-as-stof-mag-as-beeld]] warns about: repeating
content is safe, but repeating an *analogy* across subjects is not automatically
safe, because the mapping may be right in one context and wrong in the other. The
marimba case is exactly this — a ruler over a table edge is a bar and maps correctly,
a guitar string is not and does not.

## The general form

**Do not narrow a lesson to avoid saying something another subject says.** Ask
whether the repetition helps the learner, not whether it is duplicated somewhere.
The old instinct — treat overlap as waste — was mine, not Drico's.
