**Gr 6 Social Sciences, Mapungubwe, 29 September 2026.** Three fact checkers in one batch
hit a hard session limit on their web tools partway through. All three handled it well, and
between them they showed a hole in how I read their reports.

**`onseker` normally means one thing and had come to mean two.** Its real sense is *sources
conflict and a person must decide* — the first-town dispute between Huffman and Chirikure is
a true `onseker`. But after the outage it also held claims marked `onseker` **because the
checker could not reach a source at all** and refused to confirm from memory. One checker
wrote ONGETOETSTE into each source field and said in as many words: *"its `onseker` bucket is
mostly a tooling outage, not a set of genuinely contested claims — and it would be a mistake
to treat it as an honest uncertainty bucket."*

**Why this matters in both directions.** Read as real uncertainty, those items would be
escalated to Drico as curriculum decisions when they are nothing of the kind — cheap lookups
like whether stone walls still stand at Great Zimbabwe. And the lesson would carry a
MENS_NODIG verdict that looks considered, while the claims underneath it were never checked
by anybody.

**How to apply.**
- When a report comes back `MENS_NODIG`, read *why* each `onseker` item is unsettled before
  acting. A conflict between named sources is a decision; "could not reach a source" is
  unfinished work.
- A lesson whose check was cut short needs a **fresh full check**, not a re-check of the
  findings — its clean items mean nothing either, because the checker stopped mid-pass.
- One checker wrote **no report at all** for its second lesson rather than one that "looks
  like a check and is not one", reasoning that a missing file is unambiguous where a written
  verdict would not be. That was the right call and is the standard to hold: an absent report
  is better than a hollow one.
- The gap is a property of the harness, not the agents, so it will recur. Ask for the reason
  behind every `onseker`, and record in the brief when a check is a resumption.

Related: [[n-verslag-bestaan-nie-omdat-die-agent-so-se]] — its mirror image. There the risk
was assuming a report exists because an agent said so; here it is assuming a claim was
tested because a report exists. And [[n-agent-wat-faal-kan-sy-werk-klaar-he]], which is the
same class: measure what actually happened rather than trusting the status.

## A re-check overwrites the citation that settled a claim, and then 'unsettled' looks like news

**8 October 2026.** Round one of a fact check confirmed that goods were carried on foot,
citing a named 2024 paper on human porterage and down-the-line exchange over a thousand
kilometres. Round two, on a revised draft, **could not reach a source for the same claim**
and filed it unsettled — honestly, saying the general porterage literature it did reach
was West African and later, and declining to infer a thirteenth-century mechanism from it.

**A check that did not reach a source does not overturn one that did.** I nearly briefed a
writer to hedge a sentence that had already been confirmed with a citation, because the
report on disk said unsettled and the report on disk is the artefact.

**The structural part: a re-check overwrites the previous report in place.** So round
one's citation is simply gone from the live file. What survives is the archived copy in
the log tree — which nothing links to, and which nobody reads unless they know to.

**How to apply.**

* **When a re-check marks something unsettled, ask whether an earlier round confirmed it**
  before treating it as a new finding — and go to the archived report for the citation.
  [[n-verslag-in-die-logboom-kan-verouderd-wees]] says an archived report can be stale
  about the *draft*; this is the other direction: it can be the only record of the
  *evidence*.
* **The reason matters more than the status.** "Sources conflict" from a round that read
  the sources beats "confirmed" from one that did not; "not reached" beats nothing at all.
  **Rank the reasons, not the verdicts.**
* **In the re-run brief, name the claims an earlier round confirmed and with what**, so the
  new checker can confirm or contradict the actual source rather than starting from
  silence. Otherwise each round re-discovers the same thin patch and the claim oscillates.
