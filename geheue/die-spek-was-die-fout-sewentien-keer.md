Gr 5 Lewensvaardighede, 18-19 Sep 2026: across 32 lessons and 64 checks, seventeen faults turned out to live in the specification, not in the draft. Examples: the spec ordered "an adult helps you phone Childline" in four places; it defined a violent situation as people hurting "each other"; it said paraffin's contents do not look like a drink; it demanded "no number of days" for when TB stops being infectious, which made the sentence false; it required every named substance to be called illegal when glue is a shop product; it banned the rehydration recipe with a prohibition that was itself untrue.

**Why:** a draft fix alone does not hold — coverage tests against the spec, so a corrected draft then FAILS, and the next revision writes the fault back in. Writers flagged nearly all of these themselves, in the form "I fixed the lesson; the spec still orders the error."

**How to apply:** when a checker finding names a claim, grep the spec for it before briefing the writer, and sweep every field that repeats it ([[n-spek-se-dieselfde-ding-in-twee-velde]]). Amend the field's OPENING LINE, never append a note ([[n-feiterisiko-is-nie-n-regstelling-nie]]). Assert every swap and re-read the field as prose afterwards ([[n-laslap-binne-n-sin-breek-die-veld-as-prosa]]). And take a writer's "this needs fixing at source" seriously every time — it was right every time.

**30 September 2026: a sentence that no requirement ordered was invisible to the check
meant to protect it — and that is why it got cut.**

Lesson 4 of Gr 6 SW Democracy carried one sentence saying a right is **enforceable**:
whoever's right is trampled can go to court. It is the answer to the question a learner
asks next, and the standing rebuttal to the picture two withdrawn titles had carried — that
a right only works once people do their part.

A later round needed words for a legal correction, and the writer cut it. Correctly, by its
own lights: it looked for what the requirements ask, found nothing asking for that sentence,
and took the only unrequested prose in the block.

**Coverage tests the draft against `kern`. A sentence no `kern` item orders is not surplus
to coverage — it is invisible.** The check whose whole job is noticing that something
required has gone missing cannot notice a sentence nothing requires. So the protection runs
exactly backwards for the sentences that arrived because a fact check asked for them, or
because a person decided they mattered: those are the load-bearing ones, and they are the
ones with no requirement behind them.

It happened twice in one lesson. When the sentence was restored, the order for it went into
the budget-redistribution field that paid for it — where coverage cannot see it either. A
coverage checker spotted that and said so.

**How to apply.**

* **When a fact check or a decision puts a sentence into a lesson, put the order into
  `kern` in the same breath.** Not into a fact-risk note, not into a record, not into the
  field that explains why — into the item coverage tests against.
* When a writer reports cutting "confirmed prose that no requirement asks for", read what
  it cut before accepting the trade. That phrase describes both a flourish and the most
  important sentence in the lesson, and nothing downstream tells them apart.
* A cosmetic spec edit costs a finished coverage check a re-run, so this port is worth
  doing **at the moment the sentence is added**, not later.
