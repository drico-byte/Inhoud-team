8 September 2026, Gr 4 SW. The steam lessons had a mechanism error: they explained a
boiler's power by how **big** it was, and how much steam it held. That was corrected in
three lessons and in the standard's reference example, and the corrections were verified.

A narrow comparison check then found the same fault alive in a repaired block — wearing
different clothes. The block now said that being closed means the steam's push runs the
whole **length of the cylinder** instead of stopping at a pot lid. As the checker put it:
geometry is still doing the work that pressure should do, only it has moved from the
boiler's volume to the cylinder's length. And then the sentence that matters:

> Worth watching for, because it would pass a check that only greps for "big".

**Why the disguise is the danger.** Once a phrase is named as the error, that phrase is
what gets searched for — in the drafts, in the specs, in the reference example. The
underlying *wrong explanation* is what should be searched for, and it has many phrasings:
bigger, more, longer, further, for longer. Each one lets a learner reason to "more of the
same thing" when the truth is "a different amount of the same thing does not do it — the
push is harder, not bigger or longer".

The learner's destination is the tell, not the wording. In this case, both costumes end at
the same place: *engine steam is pot steam, just more of it.* A pot lid lifts at a
fraction of one pound per square inch; a pressure cooker reaches about fifteen; a
locomotive boiler runs at two hundred to two hundred and fifty. Nothing about size or
length closes that gap.

**How to apply.**

* **When you correct a mechanism, write down the false conclusion, not just the false
  words.** Then look for anything that reaches that conclusion by another route.
* **Do not close a mechanism finding on a keyword sweep.** A grep for the rejected phrase
  proves the phrase is gone, not that the explanation is fixed. The narrow comparison
  pre-check is what actually catches these, because it asks where a learner reasoning
  onward ends up — the same reason it earned its keep in
  [[elke-sin-waar-die-prentjie-vals]].
* **Suspect the repaired block hardest.** A writer fixing a named fault reaches for the
  nearest replacement, and the nearest replacement is often the same idea one step over.
  Two of the three steam blocks did exactly this.
