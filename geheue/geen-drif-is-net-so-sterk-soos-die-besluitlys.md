Grade 7 LO, 28 September 2026. `bin/woordelysdrif.py` reported **no drift: all 34 shared terms word for word the same across 45 lessons**. Ten of those 34 had never been written into the subject-grade's decision list. A coverage checker found the first one, because a spec told writers that `middel` came "word for word from the shared list" and the term was not in the list. A sweep of the drafts against the list found the other nine — `selfbeeld` in four lessons, `stereotipe` across three sub-topics, `kliniek` in eight.

**Why:** the sweep compares lessons *to each other* and, separately, to whatever decisions exist. Lessons that happen to agree produce the same clean line as lessons a decision is holding. The standard already warns that loading **no** list prints the same as agreeing with everything — the case it does not warn about is a list that is present and missing most of the terms, which is the normal state of a young subject-grade.

**How to apply:** before quoting "no drift" as evidence, sweep the drafts for every term used in two or more lessons and check each one is actually in the decision file. Do it once per subject-grade after the drafting round, not per lesson. Two things that came out of doing it: a term can be recorded as agreed and still be wrong, so mark each one as owing a read-alone fact check; and the sweep cannot see a clash between two **headwords** for one idea — `diskrimineer` and `diskriminasie` were defined differently in one grade and no sweep could ever have reported it.
