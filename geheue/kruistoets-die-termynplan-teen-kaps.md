Standing instruction from Drico, 2026-08-21: "From now on, I am going to give you the structure per
term. You have to double check my structured plan to see if anything is mentioned in the assessment
guidelines that is not in my plan."

**What he supplies.** A table per term: theme, CAPS topic, and an "Inhoud en konsepte (les)" column
where **each row is one lesson**, plus a notes column. That column is his lesson division — the
curriculum topic stays whole and he chooses the seams. Same shape as the maths creator's video split.

**Why the check exists.** His Gr 4 NWT Term 1 plan matched the CAPS content tables exactly and still
missed something. He caught it himself with a hand-written note, or it would have shipped.

## The CAPS document contradicts itself — check all three parts

Verified in the Gr 4-6 NWT document, and this is the pattern to expect elsewhere:

1. **The term-overview table** lists FIVE topics for Gr 4 Term 1, totalling 10 weeks, including
   "Strukture van plante en diere" at 2½ weeks.
2. **The detailed content tables** specify only FOUR — Lewende en nie-lewende dinge (2 weeks), Wat
   plante nodig het om te groei (1), Habitatte van diere (2), Strukture vir diere skuilings (2½).
   Total 7½ weeks. "Strukture van" appears **exactly once in the whole document**, in the overview,
   with no content section anywhere.
3. **The term's assessment guidelines** expect six capabilities, two of which no content table
   teaches: "identifiseer en beskryf die dele van 'n plant" and "identifiseer en te beskryf die dele
   van 'n dier".

So plant parts and animal parts are **assessed but never specified**. Drico's note to open the
plant-growth lesson with the four main parts covers the plant half; the animal half was still
uncovered when this was written.

## The check to run, in both directions

- **Assessed but not in the plan** — most dangerous, a learner is examined on it.
- **In the content tables but not in the plan** — same class of miss, did not occur that time.
- **Named in the overview with no content anywhere** — a hole in the document, not in his plan. Flag
  it so he decides deliberately rather than discovering it later.
- **In the plan but nowhere in CAPS** — not a defect. Record as `aanvulling` with a justification,
  the way the maths creator's "what 3D stands for" was handled.

Output a **coverage map**: every curriculum item, from all three parts of the document, against the
lesson that claims it. Produce it BEFORE any lesson is written.

**A correction worth keeping.** I first told him plant structure "is in CAPS" as though he had
overlooked a specified topic. Wrong — it is named once and never defined. His own phrasing, "al is
dit nie in caps nie", was closer to right than my correction: it is not in the content tables, only
in the assessment. Do not tell him CAPS specifies something without checking that it has a content
section, not merely a mention.

**The limit to state each time:** this checks the plan against what the document says. If CAPS itself
omits what a teacher would consider essential, only his specialists will catch that.
