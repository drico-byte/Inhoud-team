Drico read a plain-language explanation of energy I wrote for him, said that was the
level he wants, and set the standard: **the explanations must be that good, hitting
the CAPS requirements, pitched at Grade 4.** He noted himself that his own background
knowledge was doing work while he read, so the method transfers and the analogy does
not.

**Why it matters here:** coverage and explanation are different tests and only
coverage has a checker. A block can contain everything the specification asked for
and leave a nine-year-old nothing. Nothing in the pipeline catches that.

What made the difference, now written into the writer prompt as its own section:
start from an observation the learner already accepts rather than from the definition;
build in an order where each step needs the one before it; say the genuinely
surprising thing out loud instead of burying it; put the definition where it is
earned rather than where the topic is introduced; and answer the confusion the reader
already arrived with.

The load-bearing part: **this costs no words.** Order is free. A 55-word block is
five sentences either way — the question is which five and in what order. So it does
not trade against the budget, and it is not licence for a run-up.

**How to apply:** it does not override the depth ceiling for a term-dense bullet with
five named items — see [[skrywer-oorlewer-nie-onderlewer-nie]] for what happens when
a block outgrows its share. And never manufacture a surprise where the content has
none; an invented one reads as padding and a coverage checker will call it a claim
nothing asks for.
