**Gr 6 Social Sciences, 29 September 2026.** Seven specs told writers the shared-wordings
list held "three" or "four" settled terms — one said it was deliberately empty — and named
as *still open* terms that were already decided. Two writers reported it independently. A
real fault: a writer reading it would write a second wording for a settled term.

**So I prepended a dated correction to all eight specs. It said the list now holds 30
settled wordings, and it named the two terms that had been withdrawn as false.**

Within the hour I settled both of those terms and the count reached 40. My own correction
was then telling writers to replace wordings that were by that point correct, and naming
as withdrawn two terms that were settled. A writer caught it and reported the injected
list and my note contradicting each other.

**The rule: a note that freezes a state goes stale exactly as fast as the one it replaces.**
I fixed a stale-count note by writing a stale-count note. The replacement now names **no
count and no term** — it says only that the injected list is the sole authority, that a term
present in it is mandatory and word for word, and that a term absent from it is not yet
settled. That sentence stays true through every future change.

**How to apply.** When correcting a field that went stale because it recorded a *state*,
never write the current state into the correction. Point at whatever holds the state live —
the injected list, the file, the script's output — and describe the *rule* for reading it.
If I catch myself typing a number, a count, or a list of names into a note that will be read
later, that is the moment to stop: those are the things that rot. A rule does not.

**The same shape, from the same day:** the runner's own profiler line was right to say a
config's *name* is not a budget's provenance; and the spec fields that said "the wording
will be reconciled after drafting" were fine as a rule and wrong as a state once the
reconciliation had happened.
