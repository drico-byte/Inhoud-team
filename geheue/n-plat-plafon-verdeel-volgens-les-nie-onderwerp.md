**7 October 2026.** Asked whether to redo subjects made under the old method, I argued no:
the year totals of old and new land close together, so little would change. The user said it
differed more than I thought. Laying Grade 6 NWT out from CAPS at 150 words per teaching hour
proved them right: the year total was only 18% apart, but six topics with 2–3½ CAPS weeks had
each been given ONE lesson of ~540 words — 39–64% of what their hours buy — while half-week
topics got the same 540.

**Why:** the old requirements basis put a flat 500–550 band on every lesson, so a lesson's length
was set per lesson, not per topic. A matching total hides that completely.

**How to apply:** compare old and new per CAPS topic (hours vs words), never per year or per
term. Subjects planned on a flat band (Gr 5 and Gr 6 NWT, Gr 4–6 Life Skills, Gr 7 LO) likely
carry the same shortfall. Short is not the same as uncovered — coverage passed — so the remedy
is extra lessons on the starved topics, keeping the lessons that fit.

**Outcome, same day — Drico: "lets not touch gr6 science".** Checked against the content column
only ([[inhoud-kolom-plus-assessering]]), all six short lessons were complete; and his textbook
count for circuits came to ~640 words against our 547 (~90 words per teaching hour — the hours
go to hands-on work). Grade 6 NWT stays as delivered. So a word shortfall against hours × rate is
a QUESTION, not a verdict: check the content column and a hand count before calling a lesson thin.

Calibration from the same session: Drico hand-counted a Gr 6 NWT textbook for three Term 1
topics, ~155 words per teaching hour overall (~125 per printed hour, matching Gr 4 NWT's own
rate), so science is set at **150 per teaching hour**, assessment hours deducted. The book
varied hugely by topic (photosynthesis ~55/h, food groups ~290/h). We take its overall rate,
never its topic split — see [[n-eie-verdeling-mag-nie-na-die-handboek-toe-skuif-nie]] and
[[begroting-uit-kaps-ure]].
