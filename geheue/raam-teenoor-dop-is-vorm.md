18 September 2026. Drico asked what the factually correct way is to tell a crab's shell
from a backbone animal's frame. I answered "hard parts inside the soft parts, versus hard
parts outside around them". Then, when that sorted a ribcage as a shell, I answered "skin
over it or not". **Both answers sort an inner skeleton from an outer one, which is a
different distinction.** They failed on the ribcage, then on the tortoise (a bone shell
with horny plates on the outside) and on a deer's bare antler.

**The correct distinction is form, as for every structure in CAPS Technology.** A
frame is separate stiff parts (struts) with open space between them. A shell is one
continuous wall that carries the load itself. A crab's shell stays whole at its joints
(a thin, bendable membrane with no gaps), so it is a shell. Part of a frame skeleton
may be shell-shaped (the skull; a tortoise has both), and the lesson's claim is about
the skeleton as a whole.

**Why it matters:** the question was framed as biology, so I answered with biology's
endo/exo split. The lesson is really about the Technology concept, and the tin-can
sentence in the same block had the right test all along.

**How to apply:** when a lesson sorts things with a borrowed concept (structures,
materials, energy), take the discriminator from the concept's home subject. Test any
sorting rule against the awkward cases (ribcage, skull, tortoise, snail) before writing
it into a spec. Tell Drico plainly that the earlier answer was wrong.
