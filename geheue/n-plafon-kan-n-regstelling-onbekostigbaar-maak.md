A writer came back saying the repairs from a fact check do not fit — about 52 words over the
ceiling — and that there was no route down that did not cut a requirement. It stopped rather
than choosing, which was right. The cluster had no slack either: all five lessons were sitting
between their own budget and their own ceiling, 1891 measured against a 1750 envelope.

Two of the requirements it could not pay for turned out to be mine, not the findings': a
sentence about special majorities that the finding never asked for (and that the lesson's own
scope list forbids), and the by-law half of a distinction that is belt-and-braces once the
study-text sentence limits itself to an Act of Parliament. 23 words, and cutting them removes
nothing true and leaves nothing false behind. The other 30 moved from a neighbouring lesson
inside the envelope, which its own coverage round had just made room for.

**Why:** we never keep a false claim, so a fact repair cannot be the thing that gets cut to fit
a ceiling — see [[drie-maniere-om-n-bewering-reg-te-maak]]. But the pressure to cut lands on
whatever is cheapest, and the cheapest thing is rarely the least necessary. The requirements
that grew past the budget are usually the ones I wrote while porting a finding, which is exactly
where [[moenie-n-regstelling-verder-vat-as-die-bevinding-nie]] says the overreach lives.

**How to apply:** when a lesson will not fit, re-read the fields that cost the words and ask of
each one whether a fact check ordered it or I did. Cut my own gloss first, then redistribute
inside the cluster envelope (a redistribution, not an increase — say which lesson can afford it
and why), and only then raise the question of a requirement. Budgets come from
[[begroting-uit-kaps-ure]]; a writer that stops and prices the decision item by item has done
the right thing and should not be sent back to choose.
