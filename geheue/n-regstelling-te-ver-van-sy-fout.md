A false set-reading — that a valley's people were one continuous community whose centre
merely moved — survived FOUR fact checks. Each round I repaired the wording and each round
it came back. The fourth check finally named the mechanism, and none of it was wording:

* The first block closed with "**their** life changed", which made the valley's people a
  single subject for every change in the lesson.
* My repair clause said some of **these people** moved in from elsewhere — so it added
  arrival but introduced no second group, and **nobody ever left**. A population that only
  gains members is still one set.
* It sat four blocks and ~250 words BEFORE the seam it had to inform.
* The narrating block's own CLOSING sentence said the same valley and same river repeatedly
  got a new centre, which overrode the single discontinuity marker earlier in that same
  block and left the centre's *location* as the only variable.

**How to apply.** When a reading survives a repair, stop editing sentences: the variable is
placement. Put the discontinuity where the seam is narrated, not in the block where the
topic was introduced. Read each block's LAST sentence as the summary it functions as — a
closing line can cancel a marker that stands above it in the same block. And check that a
repair introduces the whole relation: arrival without departure, growth without loss, a
newcomer without an incumbent, all leave the original reading untouched. I now keep a
standing order in the field itself: if it returns a fourth time, the container is wrong and
the block order or carrier placement changes, not the prose. Related:

[[n-regstelling-ontwrig-sy-bure]], [[n-grens-kan-self-die-fout-wees]].

**12 October 2026, a FIFTH round, and the check named both the engine and my method error.**

**The engine was the curriculum's own shape.** The lesson delivers three ordered changes —
bigger, more organised, more complex — and each of its three blocks closed by saying that SO
the society in the valley became more ——, after a first block that ended on the same phrase.
Four closes on one continuous subject land before any discontinuity appears in the text. No
sentence is false; the refrain itself builds the reading. A curriculum's list of three can
manufacture a false subject simply by being delivered as three parallel conclusions.

**And the method error: I had been repairing the carriers ONE AT A TIME for four rounds.**
The check listed four and said they must land together. That is the rule to keep: when a
reading has several carriers, enumerate all of them and fix them in ONE revision, because
each surviving carrier is enough to hold the reading up on its own and a partial repair looks
like progress. I also pre-committed the escalation this time rather than repeating the loop:
if it survives a sixth round once the carriers are fixed together, the refrain is the
container, changing it touches how the curriculum's three changes are delivered, and that is
a person's decision and not a writer's.
