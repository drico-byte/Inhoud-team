29 September 2026, Gr 7 Lewensoriëntering, the reporting duty for child sexual
abuse. On 28 September a check read the consolidated statute and settled it: the
section was replaced in whole with effect from 31 July 2022, the duty binds
**every person**, and the trigger is knowing, reasonably believing **or merely
suspecting**. It recorded itself as settled, said do not reopen, and gave the
reason — the narrowing rests on a 2008 journal article, and a 2008 source cannot
describe a 2022 replacement.

On 29 September a second check narrowed it anyway: knowledge alone for every
person, suspicion only for the professions another Act names. I accepted it,
ordered the narrowing at source, and a writer put it in the draft. I propagated
it through five fields of one lesson and it reached a second lesson too.

It was wrong on both axes. Suspicion alone triggers the duty on every person,
and the other Act's trigger — reasonable grounds to conclude — is a **higher**
bar than suspicion, not the lower one my ruling assigned it. That Act's list
**adds** a duty for those professions; it takes nothing from anyone else. The
lesson's original wording had been right all along, and the narrowed draft told
a child that an adult who merely suspects need not act.

**What I actually did wrong.** I treated the later check as the better one
because it was later. But a check's date says nothing about its source's date,
and the repealed provision *was* child-specific and *did* require knowledge
alone — so every pre-2022 summary describes it accurately and reads as current
law. Search summaries hand back the old wording and the old subsection numbers.
A government department's own resource page still carries the 2007 text. This
was the **third** time that dead provision came back wearing a different
source's clothes, and the field I was editing said so in writing.

**How to apply.** When a finding contradicts one already recorded as settled,
do not resolve it on which is more recent. Ask what the new one's source is and
whether that source could have known about the change — and where the earlier
record names the trap, treat naming the trap as evidence, not as noise. For
anything that turns on a statute, settle it out of the gazette text and the
commencement proclamation, and make the check report every source's date.

**How I handled the reversal.** Both sides suspended in every field first, in
both directions, so no writer could act while it was open; a targeted check with
both positions quoted to it and the trap spelled out; then the ordering sentence
of each of five fields amended, not a note appended. See
[[die-korrigeerde-opdrag-bly-in-die-veld-staan]].
