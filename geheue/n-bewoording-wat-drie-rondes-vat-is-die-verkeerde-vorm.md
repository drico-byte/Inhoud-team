Grade 7, 23 September 2026. Three shared wordings — groepsdruk, MIV, gebalanseerde dieet — each needed three to four read-alone checks, and **every single fault arrived in the repair, not in the original**. The repair kept trading the half that worked for the half being fixed:

- *potensiaal*: removing the promise ("sal") also removed the "nog nie" that separated it from *vermoë*, leaving a circular sentence.
- *groepsdruk*: the clause added to keep bullying out ("bang om uitgelaat te word") **is** the defining mechanism of social bullying.
- *MIV*: "kom nie voor in speeksel nie" is false — the virus is present in tiny amounts; it does not **transmit** there. "Deur", never "in".
- *gebalanseerde dieet*: four rounds, and each one bought a food group by deleting a guideline's qualifier ("baie", "maer", "droë", "skoon, veilige").

**Why:** a glossary entry read alone has to be true, distinguishing and short, and past a certain content load those three stop fitting in one sentence. The diet entry only stopped failing when I changed the **form** — the entry states the principle, the seven groups moved into the lesson body where they have room to carry their qualifiers.

**How to apply:** test a correction against the case the *previous* version got right, not only against the finding. And when a wording fails a third time, stop rewriting the sentence and ask whether it is the wrong container. Say "good enough to ship though not perfect" out loud — I now ask checkers for that verdict explicitly.
