Standing rule from Drico, 2026-08-20, given as "Replace it with another example" and then
"also do that in the future with a similar situation."

**The situation it covers.** A lesson has to classify something, and there is no fact to settle
it — only competing conventions. In the Gr 4 shelter lesson: is a bird's nest a frame structure or
a shell structure? South African Grade 4 material calls it a shell; the general definition of a
frame (parts joined with gaps between them) fits it just as well, and the physics literature
describes a nest as interlocked sticks held by friction rather than a continuous skin. Woven
baskets and beehives are classed as shells, a spider web as the classic frame. Genuinely
undecidable.

**The rule: replace the contested example with an uncontested one.** Do not pick the side that
looks better argued. Do not pick the side the local textbooks use. Do not put a hedge in the
lesson — no "some people say", no "it can be seen as either". The lesson says **nothing** about
the contested case, and a clean example does the teaching instead. In this case the nest stayed in
the lesson as a shelter and simply lost its classification; a thorn-branch kraal fence became the
second frame example.

**Why, and this is what makes it different from ordinary uncertainty:** the cost is not a wrong
statement, it is a learner marked wrong in a test for confidently answering what our lesson
taught. Hedging in the lesson does not fix that — a learner still has to answer something. Only
removing the case from the lesson removes the trap.

**How to apply.** When a fact checker returns `onseker` on a classification and its reasoning is
"there is no fact here, only a convention", do not escalate the choice of side and do not spend
rounds on it. Check that the requirement can be met by a different example, swap it, and report
afterwards. Escalate only if no clean example exists, because then the requirement itself is at
risk.
