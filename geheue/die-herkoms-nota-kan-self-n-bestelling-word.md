**1 October 2026.** A writer found, inside the draft's own provenance note, a
standing instruction saying a short/full name mismatch was deliberate and must
not be harmonised. It had been written by an earlier writer **who could not see
the agreed wording it contradicted** — three requirement fields and the subject's
decided list all required the full form. So the note was issuing an order against
a ruling it had never been shown. The writer withdrew it explicitly, in capitals.

**Why this is its own failure and not just the familiar one:** the stale-order
fault we keep finding lives in a requirement field, and coverage reads those. A
standing order inside a provenance note is read by **nothing**. Coverage checks
the draft against the spec, the fact checker never sees the note at all, and no
tool compares a note against the decided wordings. It is the one place an order
can accumulate completely unchecked — and it is also the place writers are told
to record their reasoning, so it cannot simply be emptied.

**How to apply.** When briefing a writer on a lesson with a long history, say
explicitly that a "this is deliberate, do not change it" line in the note may
predate a decision it conflicts with, and that it should be checked against the
injected list rather than obeyed. When a writer withdraws such a line, make sure
the withdrawal reads as a withdrawal **from the top** — see
[[n-teruggetrekte-beslissing-bly-in-hoofletters-staan]] and
[[die-korrigeerde-opdrag-bly-in-die-veld-staan]].

## And a note's stale claim becomes a false work item for ME

**1 October 2026, the fifth instance that evening.** A note recorded that a
decided wording was too long to be protected automatically, because the tool
dropped wordings over twenty words. A coverage check read that, repeated it, and
I briefed an agent to write the protection. **The limit had been removed two days
earlier and the removal was committed.** The wording was already fully protected.
Writing the entry would have printed the word twice under two justifications and
matched 26 lesson drafts across other grades, including separately-decided
wordings that must stay as delivered.

**Why this one is worse than the writer cases:** the note did not mislead a
writer, it misled *me*, through a checker that was right to quote what it read.
A stale claim about how a tool behaves is indistinguishable, in a note, from a
live one — and notes are where we deliberately accumulate claims nothing
re-checks.

**How to apply:** a work item whose justification traces to a note is a claim,
not a finding. **Verify the mechanism before briefing the fix**, and say in the
brief that the premise is second-hand and may be stale. Four protections in one
evening were stopped by measuring first; this was one of them.
