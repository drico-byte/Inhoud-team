6 October 2026. Grade 7 LO's body-privacy lesson reached **1078 words against an original
plan of 621** after nine fact checks, with four of nine blocks at roughly double the layout
guide. Coverage had twice confirmed nothing in it was unrequested, so there was nothing to
cut without dropping a dated safety correction, and the budget had already been lifted
twice. Lampies ruled: split it.

**What the split bought, which I did not expect.** Both halves — 387 and 690 words — landed
under the grade's 750 ceiling, so **neither needs a ceiling exception any more.** Nine rounds
of granted exceptions existed only because the two halves were in one container. If a lesson
has carried an exception through several rounds and keeps growing, splitting may remove the
exception rather than merely redistribute it.

**The trap: an aggregate register measure hides a failure in one half.** The undivided lesson
passed the comma rule. The safety half alone **failed it hard** — 0.46 commas per sentence
against a 0.35 maximum — because the five gentler blocks had been diluting the average. The
split *exposed* the density rather than causing it: those blocks were always comma-dense.

So when a lesson is split, **re-gate each half and expect a failure the whole never showed.**
And the repair has a limit worth writing into the spec: bring the density inside the measure
by **breaking long sentences into separate ones, never by dropping a caveat, a hedge or a
member of a set.** A sentence enumerating a set may come apart, as long as no member
disappears.

**A requirement's halves can live in different lessons.** I apportioned all 26 requirements by
which blocks they govern, asserted that every one landed, and still got one wrong: the
requirement about respecting body changes *also* ordered the sentence that a body stays
private even when the change is visible, plus the `privaat` glossary entry — both of which
travelled with the other half. The text was right in both lessons; only my apportionment was
wrong. **Read each requirement for more than one order before assigning it**, and give a
crossing requirement to both entries with mirrored scope lines saying which half applies
where.

**How to run a split.**

1. **Renumber first, descending**, to free the slot — files, extracts, fact copies, log
   directories and every text reference, so no step catches what the previous one just
   created. Dry-run it, then verify drafts, extracts, copies and spec numbers all line up.
   Check first whether anything in the renumbered range is signed off; if nothing is, no
   reprint or re-delivery is needed.
2. **Take the seam from the measured blocks**, not from a judgement about themes, and check
   both halves clear the band floor.
3. **Both halves keep their own help route.** A safety lesson without one is a defect, and a
   split must not leave either half without one.
4. **Keep requirements superseded by the split itself as record** — the item that ordered
   "split the block, not the lesson", and the budget lift of the undivided lesson. Their
   reasoning is what explains where the block boundaries fall.
5. **Tell the writer not to rewrite.** The text carries the dated corrections; it moves word
   for word, and only antecedents broken by the separation get repaired. A writer that checks
   every back-reference and reports *nothing broke* has done the job right.
