Drico, 2026-08-28, mid-flight on Gr 4 NWT Term 4:

> "If a phrasing makes it look like its going to be factually incorrect, change it. If
> something is written in a way such that you feel its not factually correct, make it
> factually correct - however still adhere to caps. Be SMART. Remember the main goal: We
> want to create content that is factually correct, understandable by the learners and at
> the same time follows caps."

**What this changes.** Wording that risks reading as false is mine to fix, not his to rule
on. Stop sending up questions of the form "the number is right but the picture invites a
wrong sum" or "this phrase has two readings and one is false" — decide, fix, report in one
line. The escalations that remain his are real trade-offs: curriculum scope, what a lesson
teaches, changes to the standard.

**The three constraints hold together, in this order.** Factually correct. Understandable
at the grade. CAPS-aligned. Where CAPS's own wording is wrong or misleading, keep what CAPS
requires and fix how it is said — that is what was already done for the Moon's water and
for "gemaak van rots". CAPS decides *what* is taught; it does not license saying something
false.
