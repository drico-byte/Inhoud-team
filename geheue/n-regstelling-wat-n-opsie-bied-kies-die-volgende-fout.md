**Gr 4 Geography, map skills lessons 7 and 8, 5 October 2026.** Lesson 7 took six
revision rounds and lesson 8 four. In almost every one, the next fact finding was
caused by my own previous fix — and three of them share one shape that the existing
notes do not name.

**The shape: I closed a fault by writing an OPTION into the spec.** "Either say
nothing, or say X." The writer picked X, correctly and in good faith, X was true as
a sentence, and X was the next finding.

* Antarctica's ocean clause. I forbade "the Southern Ocean lies *between* Africa and
  Antarctica" (false — it begins at 60°S) and offered: say nothing, or call it the
  ocean *around* Antarctica. The writer took the second. True. But the clause sat in
  the slot its two siblings fill with "across the Atlantic" and "across the Indian",
  so it read as what singles Antarctica out — and being surrounded by ocean does not,
  because Australia two lines above is equally surrounded.
* Then I removed that clause, leaving two lines naming water and three silent. The
  three silent ones read as continents Africa simply *touches*. True of Asia, false
  of Europe, badly false of Antarctica.
* The island rule. I ordered the shared definition's second allowed wording. True,
  and applicable — but joined with "en", which lets the second condition read as a
  consequence.

**Why an option is worse than an instruction here.** When I write "either A or B" I
am reasoning about the sentence in isolation, where both really are fine. The writer
is also reasoning about the sentence, because that is what I asked. **Nobody is
reasoning about the set** — and in a parallel set the sentence is not the unit of
meaning. So the option quietly delegates the only judgement that matters to a reader
who has not been pointed at it.

**What to do instead.**

* **Decide, do not offer.** If I can see two acceptable forms, I have not finished
  thinking. Work out which one survives beside its siblings and order that one.
* **If I genuinely cannot decide, say what the choice has to survive**, not just
  what it must avoid: "whatever you write here, every one of the five lines must
  carry the same kind of thing." That is the instruction that would have prevented
  all three.
* **Uniformity beats distribution.** Twice I tried to fix an asymmetry by moving a
  clause or adding one elsewhere, and each move created the next singleton. The
  stable state of a parallel set is every item carrying the same shape — which in
  the end meant all five lines carrying a direction and nothing else.

And the diagnostic from [[twee-vereistes-wat-net-saam-vals-is]] applies: when round
three gives the opposite fault to round one — first too little water, then too much —
a constraint of mine is making the space empty, and the thing to change is the
constraint, not the wording.
