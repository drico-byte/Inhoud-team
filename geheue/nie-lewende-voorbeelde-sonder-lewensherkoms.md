Discovered on 2026-08-20, in Gr 4 NWT "Lewende en nie-lewende dinge" lesson 2, and it cost
two checking rounds and one reversal by the fact checker itself.

**The trap.** The lesson teaches a two-question sorting test: did it come from something that
lived? If no, non-living. If yes, can it still get food or grow? If not, dead. Any object
offered as a *non-living* example must therefore answer **no** to the first question — not just
be non-living in fact, but survive the test the lesson itself hands the learner.

A plastic ball fails. The same grade teaches that plastics are made from coal and oil; a later
grade teaches that coal and oil are the remains of prehistoric plants and animals. A learner
holding both links runs the test and gets **dead**, which is wrong, and gets there by using the
method correctly.

**Why "the learner does not know that yet" is not a defence.** It was the argument for naming
the ball as plastic in the first place, and it only postponed the failure: the first link is
taught in the *same* grade, same subject, same year. A learner who later acquires the second
link retroactively breaks the lesson. This is the leather-ball trap one link longer.

**Why a trace amount is different from a whole object.** Stainless steel contains fossil
carbon as a trace, and a metal spoon passes. In a plastic ball the fossil-origin material is
essentially the entire object. Depth of the chain is not the test; what the object *is* made of
is.

**Clean and unclean at Grade 4:**

- Clean: metal, stone, clay and ceramics — clay comes from weathered rock with no second link.
- Thin thread, acceptable if needed: glass — the limestone in the mix comes from sea creatures'
  shells.
- Not clean: plastic, and anything else whose raw material is former life.

**How to apply:** when picking a non-living example, run it through the lesson's own sorting
questions before writing it, not after. And never fix this by narrowing the test — every
narrower wording pushes fungi or dead wood back out on the wrong side. Replace the object.
