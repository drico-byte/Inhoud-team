7 September 2026. A fact check on Gr 4 SW contradicted *"'n ou pot wys waar mense gewoon
het"* — the information is in the findspot, not the object — and in the same breath
confirmed *"'n ou pot wys waarvan mense geëet het"*, noting only in passing that the food
evidence comes from laboratory residue analysis rather than from looking at the pot. A
writer spotted that those are the same structure and said the report could not have it
both ways. I agreed with the writer.

**Drico drew the line properly:** *"Remember that this is grade 4... So we are trying to
keep it basic... We are not telling them here that the pot first goes to the lab and only
there do the residue etc tell us some things."*

## The distinction

**Compressing the METHOD is correct simplification.** "A pot shows what people ate" is
sound because the pot **is** the thing studied — the laboratory is only *how* it is
studied. Spelling out residue analysis to a nine-year-old would be worse teaching, not
more honest.

**Misattributing WHERE THE INFORMATION LIVES is an error.** "A pot shows where people
lived" fails because the information is not in the pot at all. A pot in a museum with no
record of its findspot tells you nothing about where its owner lived. That is not a
compressed method; it is the wrong object.

## How to apply

When a checker contradicts a claim of the form *"X shows Y"*, ask which of the two it is:

* Is Y genuinely a property of X, reached by some process the lesson is right to skip?
  **Keep it.**
* Or does Y live somewhere else entirely — in the context, the findspot, the comparison,
  the record — with X only the occasion for noticing it? **Re-attribute it.**

And when a report contradicts one half of a sentence while footnoting the same structure
in the other half, that is a sign the report has not made this distinction. Say so
rather than accepting both verdicts.
