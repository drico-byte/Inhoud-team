2026-09-03, Grade 4 Lewensvaardighede. Three separate lessons, three fact checks,
the same failure each time — and in all three the study text was correct. Only
the definition was wrong.

## The three

- **`reg`** — "something **every person** must get without earning it". The
  lesson's own opening block says the opposite: some rights you have because you
  are human, others apply only to children. The Constitution agrees — basic
  nutrition and shelter belong to children only, and some rights only to citizens.
- **`VIGS`** — defined without naming MIV, so it described **any** severe weakness
  of the immune system. A child with severe malnutrition or leukaemia has that and
  does not have AIDS.
- **`jaloesie`** — "the frightened feeling that you are losing someone who matters
  to you". No rival in it, so it is not jealousy at all but plain fear of loss —
  and it equally described a grandmother falling ill, or a friend moving away.
  **Which that same lesson files under `Hartseer`.** The split had left two blocks
  describing one situation as two different emotions.

## Why it keeps happening

The writer has the whole block in front of it, so the entry reads true *there*.
The surrounding sentences supply the missing element — the child-specific rights,
the virus, the rival — and the definition inherits them without saying them.

**But nobody reads a glossary entry in place.** A learner meets it when they do
not know the word, which is precisely when they have not read or have not
understood the block. It has to carry its own meaning with nothing before it.

## The test

Read the entry with **the entire rest of the lesson deleted**. Then ask what else
in the world it fits. If it fits something the lesson calls by a different name,
it is wrong — that is what caught the jealousy one, and it is a sharper test than
asking whether the entry is true.

Watch particularly for a **missing participant**: the third party in jealousy, the
virus in AIDS, the class of person a right belongs to. A definition that drops the
element that distinguishes the concept still reads fluently, which is why it
survives the writer and the gate.

## Two related things worth carrying

The same day, the `tempel` entry took four attempts, for the opposite reason —
each version tried to make the definition do *distinguishing* work it cannot do,
since a temple differs from a church by convention, not substance. **An entry must
carry its own meaning; it need not carry a contrast.** Related:
[[die-ooreengekome-bewoording-kan-self-verkeerd-wees]].

And when a lesson splits one concept into two, **check the new entry against the
lesson's other blocks**, not only against the block it belongs to. The jealousy
entry did not collide with its own block. It collided with sadness.
