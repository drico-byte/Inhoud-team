Drico, 2026-08-25, on the Grade 4 Term 4 rocket lesson: "We are not making it
practical but rather teaching the thinking behind it... but that's anyway what CAPS
says needs to be taught. We are not focusing on anything practical. That's not our
goal. We want to teach / support the teaching of theory content. If the HTML team can
make the theory practical with a good interactive illustration, then it's a bonus."

## The correction

We had been treating practical assessment items as **excluded** — the shelter design
task, the paper struts, the balloon rocket. That is the wrong frame and it produces
thinner lessons than it should.

The right frame: **the theory was always the target.** A practical task tests
understanding of something, and that something is ours to teach. What we do not do is
walk a learner through making the object, because a text cannot.

The difference is real. Under the old frame, Term 4's rocket lesson looked like a
lesson whose whole assessment we skip. Under this one it is a lesson about why a rocket
moves by pushing gas out behind it, and what makes one balloon rocket travel further
than another — knowledge, in scope, and exactly what the making task is testing.

## How to apply

When a sub-topic's assessment is a make-and-evaluate task, do not mark the topic
out of scope. Ask what a learner has to **understand** to do that task well, and put
that in the lesson. Keep out of the lesson: step-by-step making instructions, the
measuring and recording procedure, and the bar graph.

**Interactivity is not our gap to fill.** If the layout team turns the theory into
something a learner can play with, that is a bonus on top — see Drico on that team
earlier the same day: "The html team made it practical, showing how it comes alive
anyways." Do not pad a lesson trying to substitute for it.
