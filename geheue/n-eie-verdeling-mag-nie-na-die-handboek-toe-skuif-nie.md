**29 September 2026.** My Grade 6 SW Term 1 split, derived from CAPS clusters alone, turned out
to match a publisher's ten-lesson division seam for seam — the only difference was that the book
merged my first two lessons. I never saw the book; Drico hand-counted it and gave me numbers.

**Why it matters:** selection and arrangement are what copyright protects, and matching a
publisher's LESSON STRUCTURE is the real exposure, not wording. Our protection is that the
generation chain never had access, and the git history is the evidence.

**How to apply:** landing on the same division independently is fine, and even reassuring.
**Adjusting our division because theirs differs is not** — that is taking structure from the
book, and it is the one move that undoes the protection. When a book's split differs from ours,
decide the question on OUR grounds and say what those grounds are.

Worked example: I had merged the valley-growth and K2/Schroda lessons, then split them only
because the pair broke a word ceiling. When the ceiling was dropped I merged them again — and
that lands on the book's division. The reason on the record is the content argument (a lesson
whose whole content is "society grew bigger" has nothing concrete in it), not the book.

The same discipline applies to EMPHASIS. The book gave the Indian Ocean trade 190 words; CAPS
names that trade as the topic's main focus in its own words, so we give it 380. CAPS's stated
emphasis beats a publisher's page budget.
