Grade 6 Social Sciences, Democracy lesson 1, 30 September 2026. A title carried two words the
lesson itself hedged — "the day when EVERYONE could vote", where the first block says "adults of
every population group" two sentences later and "voting ran over more than one day" four sentences
on. I settled it that morning in favour of keeping it, with three reasons, and wrote **MOENIE DIT
WEER OPPER NIE** into the spec. The sixth fact check let it through; the seventh reported it as a
contradiction. Two of seven rounds on that one title.

Two separate mistakes, worth keeping apart.

**The decision was wrong on its merits, and the evidence was already mine.** The standing rule in
[[elke-les-kry-n-eie-naam]] describes this exact case: a title may carry no unhedged universal
that the lesson itself hedges. That rule comes from Drico's own rejected titles and is older than
my ruling. I wrote a same-day decision straight past a rule I already had — the same shape as
[[ek-skryf-my-regstelling-bo-op-sy-weerlegging]], one drawer further away.

**And the instruction could never have worked.** The fact checker never sees the spec, by design —
that is the whole point of [[die-herkoms-nota-lek-die-spek-na-die-feitenasiener]]. So a note
telling it not to raise something is addressed to a reader who will never receive it, and the
point comes back every round, at the cost of a full check each time.

**Why:** a decision that a checker keeps re-raising is evidence about the text, not about the
checker. Either the text needs changing, or the defence has to live somewhere the checker can see
— which means inside the lesson, in what it actually says.

**How to apply:** when a checker raises something a spec note already settled, do not re-record
the ruling. Ask first whether a standing memory note already governs it, and then make the text
not need defending. `MOENIE DIT WEER OPPER NIE` is legitimate only for readers who see the spec —
writers and the coverage checker. Never for the fact checker. See
[[nasieners-trek-teenoorgesteld-oor-voorbehoude]] for the case where two checkers genuinely
disagree and the answer is to keep what you have.
