Grade 5 Natuurwetenskappe, 9 September 2026. Two planners ran in parallel on
neighbouring sub-topics and reached opposite conclusions about `geraamte`.

* **Dieregeraamtes** (vertebrate skeletons only) reused Grade 4's agreed wording —
  *the frame inside the body of an animal with a backbone* — and it fits perfectly.
* **Geraamtes as strukture** refused that wording, because CAPS Grade 5 calls a
  crab's shell a skeleton: not a frame, not inside, no backbone.

Each was right **for its own sub-topic**, and each said so with good reasoning.
Shipping both would have put two definitions of one word inside one grade.

**Why:** every safeguard we have looks at one lesson or one sub-topic. The gate
reads one lesson. Coverage reads one lesson against one spec entry. The drift
sweep is real but runs *after* drafting, at reconciliation. A contradiction that
lives between two sub-topics of the same grade is visible to none of them until the
lessons already exist — and by then it is a correction sheet.

**How to apply:** when planning a grade, look for terms whose SCOPE differs between
sub-topics before drafting starts, and settle those early even though the agreed-
wordings list is otherwise meant to be filled in after the drafts. The general test:
a term is safe to leave until reconciliation when every sub-topic uses it the same
way, and must be settled up front when one sub-topic applies it more broadly than
another. Running planners in parallel makes this more likely, not less — they cannot
see each other.
