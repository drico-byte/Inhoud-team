Gr 6 SW, Medisyne oor tyd heen, lesson 6, 14 October 2026. The spec's ordering line read
**"WAAROM NIEMAND DIE KIEM VOOR KOCH UITGEKEN HET — DRIE DINGE SAAM"**, and then named the
three things. Everything after the dash was checked and correct. The question itself was not:
it presupposes that nobody had recognised the germ before Koch.

The writer delivered the premise faithfully, as a flat sentence — and it is a "first" claim
without the word *eerste*, so none of the superlative rules fired on it. A fact check returned
it `onseker` on a genuine source conflict: a specialist history says another physician
identified the same micro-organism almost simultaneously, without staining, and presented it
before Koch's lecture, while Koch's own report and the reference works attribute the
identification to Koch.

**What I had actually decided** was that the lesson should explain why the germ was *hard to
recognise*. I never decided it should claim a precedence. The question form added that for free
and I did not see it, because I was reading the three grounds — the part I had argued about.

**So: a field that orders "why X" also orders X.** When amending an ordering line, read its
opening clause as a claim and ask whether it has been checked. Three forms to watch:

* a why-question (*why nobody…*, *why it was the first…*, *why it only became possible then*);
* a "what changed" framing, which presupposes that the thing before was different;
* a heading that names a cause, which presupposes the effect.

The fix was to amend the opening clause to ask for the difficulty, bar the precedence half
explicitly with its reason, and say in the same breath that the discovery attribution itself
stands — because that half *was* confirmed, and barring the claim too widely would have cost a
sentence CAPS asks for. Related: [[die-veilige-vorm-maak-die-fout]],
