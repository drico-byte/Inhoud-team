Drico, 9 September 2026, on `geraamte`.

**The ruling:** a definition **may widen across grades when the curriculum widens**,
and **never within a grade**.

**Why this is not an exception to the existing rule.** The standing rule — a
definition may not get richer as the year goes on — exists because the end-year exam
covers the whole year, so a learner looking a word up in two lessons must get one
answer. That reason is bounded by one year. It says nothing about two grades, which
have two different exams.

**The case.** Grade 4 defines `geraamte` as *die raam binne-in die lyf van 'n dier
met 'n ruggraat, wat hom ophou* — the restriction to backboned animals was added
deliberately on 30 August 2026. CAPS Grade 5 then calls a crab's shell a skeleton.
Grade 5's wording became *die dele wat 'n dier se lyf ophou en sy sagte dele
beskerm*: it keeps Grade 4's function, drops the placement and the group, and adds
protection. **Grade 4 stays exactly as delivered** — no correction sheets.

**The word `harde` may not come back.** It was removed from the Grade 4 wording after
a fact check: a shark's whole skeleton is cartilage.

**How to apply:** when a Grade 5 term collides with a delivered Grade 4 one, widening
the later grade is available and is usually right. Check first whether the curriculum
really widened, or whether we simply worded one of them badly — the ruling licenses
the first, not the second.
