Seven substantive errors in the CAPS document surfaced in Grade 5 NWT by
10 September 2026 — the candle burning "until all the oxygen is used up" (a flame
dies near 16%), slate listed as sedimentary, "plants and animals grow throughout
their lives" (false of birds and mammals), biodiversity as the *sum* of plants,
animals and habitats rather than the *variety*, leaves and stems called "hard
parts", `buigbaar` glossed as ductile, and electricity running "back to the power
station". Plus printing errors, of which the best is "permanente skade aan die
VERHOOR" in the sound lesson — *verhoor* is a court hearing.

**Drico's pattern, from three rulings in one day:**

* **A NAME or a classification: ride with CAPS.** Slate keeps CAPS's name and the
  lesson simply says nothing false about it; `buigbaar` keeps CAPS's sense. The
  exam follows CAPS, and a learner who has never met the word is worse off than
  one carrying a wrong formation story.
* **A MECHANISM the learner will reason with: correct it.** The electricity return
  path was fixed even though CAPS's bullet demands the words, because a learner
  builds on a mechanism and a cheap true form existed.
* **Where a true form covers CAPS's point anyway, just use it** and do not announce
  the disagreement — the candle says "when not enough oxygen remains"; biodiversity
  keeps all three parts CAPS names and says "the variety of".

**How to apply:** ask whether the learner will *reason further* with the claim. A
label gets filed; a mechanism gets built on. And check whether CAPS's own point
survives the true version — it usually does, which is what makes the quiet fix
possible. Related: [[drie-maniere-om-n-bewering-reg-te-maak]],
[[kaps-se-lys-wen-oor-die-handboek-se-lys]].

## 13 October 2026, Gr 6 SW Mapungubwe: "Goue renosters", plural

CAPS's own bullet for Mapungubwe lesson 4 reads *"Goue renosters en ander goue voorwerpe"*.
**There is one gold rhinoceros.** The University of Pretoria, which holds the collection,
headlines it "The one and only gold rhino in the world"; the other gold animals from the
same graves are a bovine and a feline. A fact check caught it in the lesson's `kaps_punt`
metadata, where we quote the bullet verbatim.

**What we did, and why it is the no-action case.** The lesson's own study text already says
one rhino, singular, with no number — that was settled on 7 October 2026 after an earlier
round. So the error lives only in the quoted bullet, a learner never meets it, and nothing
needs changing. The fact checker flagged it explicitly as "the writer must not change the
lesson text to match the metadata", which is the right instinct: the natural repair of a
mismatch is to make the draft match the spec, and here that would introduce the error.

**The general point this adds to the pattern above.** A faithful quotation of CAPS can carry
a CAPS error into our files without that error ever reaching a learner. Those are worth
recording and worth leaving alone — but say so in the record, because the next person to
notice the mismatch will reach for the draft. Related:
[[kaps-se-lys-wen-oor-die-handboek-se-lys]], [[n-bevinding-wat-ek-oordra-word-n-bevel]].
