I narrowed a claim in a requirement field and inserted the narrowing above two older
sentences. Both older sentences said the K2-to-Mapungubwe leg "WORD HIERDEUR NIE GERAAK
NIE" — and when they were written, "hierdeur" meant an earlier, different correction, which
made them true and useful. After my insertion a reader coming from the top met the
narrowing first, so the sentence now read "the narrowing does not touch that leg" — which
is the exact authority a reviser would cite to reinstate the claim I had just withdrawn.

**Neither sentence was edited, and neither was wrong when written.** My insertion was
correct. The fault is purely positional, which is why nothing catches it: a sweep for the
old claim finds nothing, the field contains no contradiction to match, the marker is in the
right place, and every sentence is individually defensible. It is invisible until someone
reads the field top-down as a writer does.

It also propagated before anyone saw it. A writer built a list item on that sentence and
set the two changes against each other, and the fact check named that item the lesson's
only unresolved point. So the cost was not a stale note — it was a false implication in
delivered text, arriving through a sentence I never touched.

**How to apply:** after inserting anything into a field, re-read the sentences BELOW the
insertion and resolve every pronoun and back-reference — hierdeur, daardie, dit, sy, hom,
"daardie lees", "hierdie tak" — against what now stands above them, not against what stood
there when they were written. A back-reference whose target is a dated correction is the
dangerous kind, because corrections are exactly what gets inserted above. When one has
moved, do not re-point the pronoun: name the thing outright and put the quoted old sentence
in a record BELOW the marker, because a verbatim old order above the marker is how this
fault was born in the first place. Related: [[die-korrigeerde-opdrag-bly-in-die-veld-staan]],
