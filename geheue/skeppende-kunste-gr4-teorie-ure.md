Opened 2026-09-08, before any Skeppende Kunste lesson was planned.

## Where it is in the CAPS document

Skeppende Kunste is 3.2.3. **The PDF runs five pages ahead of the printed
numbers** — printed 35 is PDF page 40 — so a viewer's page numbers land in
Liggaamlike Opvoeding if you trust them.

Grade 4 sits in **two separate blocks**, because the document is ordered by art
form first and grade second:

- **Uitvoerende Kunste, Graad 4** — printed 35-38
- **Visuele Kunste, Graad 4** — printed 47-50

Creative Arts is 1.5 hours a week, 60 hours a year: Performing Arts 40, Visual
Arts 20. The content pages and the allocation table agree, which is worth saying
because they did not for another subject.

## The theory hours: 8 of the 60

**Visual Arts — "Visuele geletterdheid", 1 hour a term.** The only topic in either
art form whose resources name a *handboek*; everything else lists clay, paint and
recyclables. Same shape every term: identify and name the art elements, then
identify and name contrast (proportion joins from Term 2), then questions to
deepen observation (Terms 2-4 only), then apply to own work — that last one is the
making handover and is not ours. Only the subject changes: the human body in
action, lettering and pattern-making, wild and domestic animals, the natural
environment. Artworks join photos and real objects as stimuli from Term 3.

**Performing Arts — "Waardeer en nadenke", 1 hour a term.** Terms 1, 2 and 3 are
**word for word identical**; Term 4 drops the first bullet. So four timetabled
hours carry one knowledge topic (classifying instruments by appearance, name, how
the sound is produced, and pitch high-low) plus one recurring skill (discussing
performances in simple arts terminology).

## RULING 1, Drico, 8 September 2026: divide by term, not by concept

I put two cuts to him. By concept teaches each thing once and properly. By term
follows the classroom calendar and repeats the frame four times.

**He chose by term**: it is the flow teachers naturally follow, and "sometimes
repetition is good in education."

So the repetition in the source is **kept on purpose**. Do not "improve" it into a
concept-ordered set later — the repetition is the ruling, not an oversight, and a
future reader looking at the Performing Arts hours will see the same content three
times and be tempted to collapse it.

## RULING 2, Drico, 8 September 2026: the term-by-term plan beats the overview

The document describes Creative Arts twice — a summary at 3.1.3 (printed 13-16)
and the full term-by-term plan at 3.2.3. **For the Grade 4 appreciation hour they
disagree.**

- **Overview:** a variety of music using non-melodic percussion **and melodic**
  instruments, **African and Western**.
- **Term-by-term plan:** the expressive qualities of **percussion** instruments in
  an **African** music piece.

Narrower on both axes. **Drico ruled for the term-by-term plan**, on this evidence:

- It is the operative plan — it carries the contact times and it is what a teacher
  works from, so our text matches the classroom.
- **It is internally coherent across the three grades, and the overview is not.**
  Grade 5's own detail says "Western **or** African music, classify the instrument
  visually and by ear", then moves on to genres and styles; Grade 6 compares two
  South African dramas, dances and music types. So Grade 4 is deliberately narrow,
  Grade 5 widens, Grade 6 compares.
- Following the overview would have Grade 4 doing Grade 5's job, leaving Grade 5
  nothing new.

## But the overview is still the authority where it FILLS A GAP

This is the distinction, and it is not arbitrary — the two cases are different.

The term-by-term plan says "die kunselemente" and never lists them. **The overview
does**, and calls it *formele onderrig* for Grade 4: **line, shape, secondary and
related colour, tints and shades**; for 3D **texture, form and colour**; plus form
in space (behind, in front, beside, above, below). Design principles: **contrast
and proportion**. Grade 5 adds complementary colour and emphasis; Grade 6 adds
monochromatic colour, balance and unity.

So: **where the overview contradicts the detail, the detail wins. Where the detail
is silent, the overview supplies it.** Both were applied here in the same session
and it would look inconsistent without this note.

## Two small oddities in the source, neither acted on

Visual Arts Term 1 lacks the "this is compulsory" sentence that Terms 2-4 carry,
and lacks the "questions to strengthen observation" bullet the other three have.
It reads like the first term was set before the pattern settled. Recorded so it is
not mistaken for something we dropped.
