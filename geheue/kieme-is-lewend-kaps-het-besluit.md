Decided by the orchestrator on 2026-08-19, under Drico's standing instruction to
decide during the unattended test run. **Flagged as reviewable** — it is a curriculum
call and he may want it differently.

**The question.** The fact checker escalated: "kieme" in ordinary usage includes
viruses (KidsHealth, Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic all list them), and whether viruses
are alive is genuinely unsettled — the Microbiology Society and Scientific American
treat it as an open question, and Cleveland Clinic says viruses are not living
organisms "however, there's some debate over this". So can a Grade 4 lesson say germs
are not dead?

**Decision: yes. Keep the word `kieme` and CAPS's framing.**

Reasons:

1. **CAPS has already decided it.** The Gr 4 Term 1 note reads: "Maak seker dat
   leerders verstaan dat kieme ook lewendig is maar te klein is om met die blote oog te
   sien." The curriculum instructs that germs are living. A lesson following that is not
   making a virology claim; it is teaching the curriculum.
2. The dispute is definitional — what counts as life — not a fact a nine-year-old
   needs, and it is unsettled among specialists.
3. Narrowing to `bakterieë` would depart from CAPS's own word and introduce a term a
   Grade 4 does not have, against the house word-choice rule of preferring the everyday
   word.

**What must change even so:** the lesson may not make a UNIVERSAL claim about germs.
"Elkeen van hulle eet en groei" is false for viruses. It becomes "Baie kieme eet en
groei", which is true and costs nothing.

**How to apply:** any lesson touching germs, microbes or disease at Grade 4 follows
CAPS and says germs are living things too small to see, and avoids universal claims
about what every germ does. Do not reopen the virus question at this grade.
