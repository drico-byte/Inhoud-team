Three times on 28 August 2026, in the same subject:

* **"about a hand's length"** for the ruler's overhang, taken from a fact
  report and written into `demonstrasie_eise` as a requirement. A hand is half a
  30 cm ruler, which gives about 25 vibrations per second — below where pitch is
  heard, and exactly the length the activity guides report as a clatter.
* **"about a third of the ruler"**, the replacement, taken from the next report
  the same way. A third is 10 cm; a nine-year-old's middle finger is about 6.5.
  Out by half. Both times the fact checker caught it, and both times only because
  the next round happened to be asked a sharper question.
* **"they now stand elsewhere in the lesson as noise sources"**, written into a
  correction note about a dog and a car alarm. They stand nowhere in the lesson.
  I asserted it about a file I had open and did not look.

**Why it is worse in a spec than in a draft.** Everything a draft says gets read
by two checkers whose whole job is doubting it. A spec is read as settled — the
writer builds from it, the coverage checker measures against it, and neither is
looking for a wrong number in the requirements. `spesifikasies-word-nooit-nagegaan`
already records that a false mechanism in a spec plants itself in every lesson;
these are the same fault in its smallest form, a single figure or clause.

**How to apply:** a number going into a spec gets checked in the spec, not
inherited from the report it came from — do the arithmetic, in units, before
writing it. A claim about what a lesson contains gets a search of the lesson
first. The report is evidence, not a source of truth, and my own memory of a file
is not evidence at all. Where a figure has already been wrong once, write the
wrong version and why into the field beside the right one, so the next reader
sees the history rather than the conclusion.
