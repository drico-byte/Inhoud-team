A fact check found the rope comparison implied the muscle sits inside the bone. I
wrote the correction into the spec as an instruction: *"Sê waarbinne die spier lê."*
The writer followed it exactly and produced *"die tou lê buite die kis, maar die spier
lê binne die dier se lyf"* — literally true, and it lands the learner in the same
wrong place, because the crate is the thing the pull moves throughout the block. It
was worse than before: the "maar" now claimed a difference at the one point where the
comparison actually holds, since the muscle, like the rope, is outside what it moves.
The narrow ELI10 pre-check caught it the same day.

**Why:** describing a fault leaves the writer free to find the shape that works;
prescribing the repair hands them my shape, and my shape has not been checked by
anything. A spec field is read as settled, so a prescriptive sentence in one is
obeyed rather than weighed. This is the same failure as [[moenie-self-inhoud-skryf-nie]]
arriving one level up — not in the lesson, but in the instruction that generates it.

**How to apply:** in a repair brief and in a spec correction, write what is FALSE and
why, plus what must survive. Do not write the replacement sentence. Where a checker
offers wording, pass it on labelled as a suggestion, never as the requirement — and
say what the checker could not see. See also [[n-regstelling-ontwrig-sy-bure]] and
[[n-feiterisiko-is-nie-n-regstelling-nie]].
