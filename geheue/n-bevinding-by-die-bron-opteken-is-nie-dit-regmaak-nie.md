A fact check contradicted two claims in a lesson's opening block. I recorded both
at source as fact-risk items, carefully, with the reasoning — and moved on. Two
rounds later the draft still carried both sentences. The spec now said the right
thing and the lesson still said the wrong thing, and its coverage was already
approved, so nothing downstream would have caught it before sign-off.

**Why:** writing the guard is the part that feels like the fix, because it is the
part that takes thought. Dispatching the writer is clerical, so it drops. The
danger is specific: a guard makes the FUTURE safe and does nothing about the
present draft, and it leaves a written record that looks like the finding was
handled.

**How to apply:** a finding is closed only when the draft has changed and gated.
Fix at source AND dispatch, in the same breath, every time — the CLAUDE.md rule
about fixing at source is an *addition* to repairing the draft, not a substitute.
When recording several findings at once, count the writers dispatched against the
lessons named before moving on. See also [[n-verslag-bestaan-nie-omdat-die-agent-so-se]]
and [[n-feiterisiko-is-nie-n-regstelling-nie]].
