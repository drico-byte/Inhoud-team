**73 lessons, 8 sub-topics**, four History and four Geography. All 73 are drafted
and gated. **One is signed off**: Democracy and citizenship lesson 3, on
1 October 2026 — the first in the subject.

**Where it stands, 1 October 2026.** In Democracy and citizenship: lesson 3 signed
off; lessons 1, 2 and 4 revised, gated and in their checks; 5 and 6 at the end of
their repair chains; **lesson 7 has six fact findings ported to the spec and no
post-repair draft**; **lesson 8 has never been checked at all**. The other seven
sub-topics — 65 lessons — are drafted and within budget and **nothing more is

them. Treat "gated" there as meaning measured, not correct.

**Zero glossary drift across the subject** as of 1 October, verified rather than

sentence needs its scope said out loud.

## The year's rulings

* **No article number of the Constitution in a learner's text, anywhere in the
  sub-topic.** CAPS asks for none at this grade. Lampies, 1 October. Bound at
  subject level in `buite_bestek`, not per lesson — it had been in one lesson's
  fields and two writers warned independently that nothing carried it. Citations
  in a spec field are provenance and never travel.
* **`reg` belongs to a person's entitlement and is never used in the law sense**
  in this subject-grade. That one ruling moved four agreed wordings in a day.
* **Grade 6 says `gelyk voor die wet`; Grade 7 says `voor die reg`.** Lampies,
  1 October: each grade keeps its own rule. Grade 7's spec requires rights worded
  as the Constitution words them; Grade 6's reserved-word ruling wins here. The
  protected-words list needs grade scoping for this — until then one entry forbids
  what the other requires.
* **A municipal by-law stays `wet`, not `verordening`.** Declined six times. A
  fact checker never sees a spec, so this cannot stop recurring; the refusal is
  recorded so a seventh raising lands as answered.
* **The four names of harm keep their four-way split** — our teaching convention,
  approved knowing no source carries four distinguishable meanings. Each entry is
  narrower than its source word, which is the allowed direction.

## What this subject costs, and why

About twenty runner rounds per lesson — near the top of everything done so far,
though three of the five most expensive sub-topics on record are Grade 4 Social
Sciences, so this is the **subject** and not this sub-topic. Life Skills ran at
half the rate: it teaches behaviour, where a fact check has little to bite on,
while this teaches dates, named people and constitutional mechanics.

**The dominant failure, twenty-two instances in one sub-topic:** a requirement's
instruction left standing after a correction below it withdrew what it ordered.
Several were mine, two made within an hour of my removing the same fault
elsewhere. See [[die-korrigeerde-opdrag-bly-in-die-veld-staan]] and
[[n-nota-wat-n-toestand-vasvries-verouder]]. The reliable detector is an agent
reading the field from the top — three attempts to mechanise it have failed and
are recorded in the sweep itself.

**Before the next sub-topic enters checking:** settle its shared wordings first,
and fact-check its requirements before anything is written. One false claim of
mine in a requirement cost about ten agent runs; checking that sentence first
would have cost one. See [[spesifikasies-word-nooit-nagegaan]].
