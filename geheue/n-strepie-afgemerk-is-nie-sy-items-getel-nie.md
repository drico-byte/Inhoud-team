**Gr 4 Geography, 29 September 2026.** My coverage map ticked *"Geboue en hul gebruike →
Les 3"* and moved on. The CAPS bullet actually reads *"geboue **soos** huise,
**diereskuilings**, winkels, skole, klinieke, banke, kantore, plekke van aanbidding,
fabrieke, motorhuise en treinstasies"* — eleven named items, and the script has ten. I
never counted them, because the row was already green.

**Why:** a map built one row per CAPS bullet produces the same confident table whether the
bullet was checked as a whole or item by item. Nothing in the artefact records which one
happened, so the next reader — including me, a week later — cannot tell a real check from
a shallow one. Same family as [[my-soektogte-mis-en-dan-glo-ek-hulle]] and
[[n-skoon-toets-is-so-wyd-soos-sy-omvang]]: the output of a narrow check is shaped exactly
like the output of a wide one.

**How to apply:** when building a coverage map, **expand every CAPS bullet into its named
items and tick those**, not the bullet. Then run the check in the other direction too —
every lesson back to CAPS — because bullet→script finds missing coverage while
script→bullet finds lessons that serve nothing, and they are different failures. Doing the
second pass here found two lessons that would have been failed as supplement and are
actually core.

**And read CAPS's own grammar, because it marks which lists bind.** *"Maniere waarop mense
hul voedsel verkry - koop; verbou; versamel, visvang, jag"* has no hedge: closed list,
every item mandatory, and that is why hunting became a ruling. *"geboue **soos** huise,
diereskuilings, ..."* has "soos": illustrative, so a missing item is worth a clause, not a
ruling. CAPS also hedges outright where it means to — *"Dit is nie nodig om al die
bogenoemde behoeftes te dek nie."* Grading a "soos" list as binding invents mandatory work;
grading a closed list as illustrative drops curriculum.
