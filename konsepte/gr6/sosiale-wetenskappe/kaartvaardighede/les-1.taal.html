1Na die ander kant lêAan die ander kant lê'Na die ander kant' is a direction phrase paired with the static verb 'lê', which reads stiffly.
Both say 90 degrees south lies on the other side of the equator, the previous sentence already carries the counting direction, and 'aan die ander kant lê' is the natural phrase.
3Sonder noord of suid en oos of wesSonder noord of suid en sonder oos of wesTwo 'of' pairs joined by 'en' under one 'sonder' are hard to parse on a first reading.
Repeating 'sonder' separates the two pairs without changing the meaning and leaves the protected clause intact.
4Met die kant by elke getalMet die rigting by elke getal'Die kant by elke getal' is vague about what is written with the number.
'Rigting' names exactly what noord, suid, oos and wes are, and the meaning stays the same.
