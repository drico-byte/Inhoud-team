#VoorNáFault and reason
1lê op die Oranjerivierlê in die Oranjerivier'lê op die Oranjerivier' copies English 'on the river'; Afrikaans normally places a dam 'in' the river it dams, and the original also stacks two 'op' phrases ('op die Oranjerivier, op die grens').
Checked the preposition: both versions say the same place (the dam sits in the river's course), the protected word Oranjerivier is untouched, and 'in' is the natural Afrikaans idiom that also removes the doubled 'op'.

Flags from the finder

Die rivier het dieselfde gebly, maar die dam se naam het verander.
The contrast is between the river and the dam's name, so a learner may wonder whether the river itself or the river's name stayed the same; making it 'die rivier se naam' would need the intended content, so it is flagged rather than changed.
