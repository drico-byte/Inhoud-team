2Die rots bo-oor hetDie rots daarbo het'bo-oor' as a post-modifier of 'die rots' is unnatural; it means 'over the top of', not 'overhead'.
'Die rots daarbo' is the natural form and keeps the meaning of the overhang protecting the paint.
5Die San het hulle verf self gemaak van kleurstowwe uit die natuur.Die San het hulle verf self van kleurstowwe uit die natuur gemaak.The participle before the source phrase copies English order ('made their paint from ...').
Putting 'gemaak' at the end is the more natural written Afrikaans order and changes nothing in meaning.
7die rots onder wasdie rots daaronder was'die rots onder' as a post-modifier is unidiomatic.
'Die rots daaronder' is the natural form and keeps the meaning exactly.
8Volgens hulle het die kunstenaarsVolgens hierdie mense het die kunstenaars'Volgens hulle' could point to the San or to the people who thought so, with a second 'hulle' for the artists in the same sentence.
'Hierdie mense' fixes the reference to the earlier thinkers without adding any information.
12'n erfenis wat alle Suid-Afrikaners deel'n erfenis wat alle Suid-Afrikaners gemeen het'deel van ... wat alle Suid-Afrikaners deel' uses 'deel' twice with two meanings in one sentence.
'Gemeen het' removes the clash and keeps the meaning that all South Africans share the heritage.
13'n bron wat ons oor die San se lewe en geloof leer'n bron waaruit ons oor die San se lewe en geloof leer'n bron wat ons ... leer' is ambiguous between the source teaching us and us learning it, and reads awkwardly.
'Waaruit ons ... leer' says plainly that we learn from the source, which is what the sentence means.
14die paneel se skuif vandie paneel se verskuiwing van'skuif' as a noun for a relocation is colloquial.
'Verskuiwing' is the standard noun and the meaning is unchanged.
